# History of Money Vertical Timeline

## Alt text

A vertical timeline showing the evolution of money from barter and commodity money through coins, paper, the gold standard, fiat and digital money, ending with Bitcoin in 2009.

## Texts

### era-1

Prehistory

### title-1

Barter

### body-1

People traded goods directly — grain for tools, livestock for cloth. It only worked when both sides wanted what the other had.

### era-2

~9000 BC

### title-2

Commodity Money

### body-2

Cattle, salt, shells and beads became widely accepted units of value — the first money that stood for more than itself.

### era-3

~600 BC

### title-3

Metal Coins

### body-3

The kingdom of Lydia struck the first standardized coins from gold and silver, stamped to certify their weight and worth.

### era-4

~700 AD

### title-4

Paper Money

### body-4

Merchants in Tang China swapped heavy coins for paper receipts. Governments soon issued the first true banknotes.

### era-5

1816

### title-5

The Gold Standard

### body-5

Paper money became a promise: every note could be redeemed for a fixed amount of gold held in a nation's vaults.

### era-6

1971

### title-6

Fiat Money

### body-6

The link to gold was cut. Money now has value only because a government says so — and it can be printed without limit.

### era-7

1990s

### title-7

Digital Money

### body-7

Cards and online banking turned money into numbers on a screen — fast, but always controlled by banks and middlemen.

### era-8

2009

### title-8

Bitcoin

### body-8

For the first time, money that is digital, scarce and open to all — issued by no company or state, and controlled by no one.

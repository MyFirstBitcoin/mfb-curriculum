# 7.14 Why the US dollar is a reserve currency

Keep this section proportionate. Explain reserve [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency), the historical role of the pound, the postwar position of the United States, [Bretton Woods](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bretton-woods), the 1971 end of official international dollar-gold [convertibility](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#convertibility), and continuing network and market effects.

Be ready to facilitate discussion, not only present the timeline. Prepare learners to ask why reserve-currency use can persist, what institutions and [network effects](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#network-effect) support it, what has changed over time, and which claims require current evidence.

Treat claims about the dollar's current reserve share as time-sensitive. Verify and date them before use. Do not turn the topic into a prediction of imminent collapse or permanent dominance.

Ask learners whether a [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) can remain widely used after one part of its original arrangement changes. This helps prepare the concept of [network effects](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#network-effect).

# 7.10 Beginner monetary history

Build a layered or braided timeline rather than a single arrow of progress. Include:

* direct exchange, gifts, obligations, and records
* [commodity money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#commodity-money)
* standardized coins and [debasement](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debasement)
* [representative paper](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#representative-paper) and [convertibility](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#convertibility)
* bank and government money
* modern fiat arrangements; and
* digital interfaces to fiat-denominated claims.

Invite local additions. Ask which forms coexisted rather than assuming one replaced another completely.

If using the [gold standard](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold-standard), [Bretton Woods](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bretton-woods), the [Nixon Shock](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#nixon-shock), or [Executive Order 6102](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#executive-order-6102):

* keep the explanation brief and relevant to the module
* state the location and historical scope
* present [Executive Order 6102](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#executive-order-6102) as a specific United States example
* present 1971 as an important marker in international dollar-to-gold [convertibility](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#convertibility), not the origin of all [fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money); and
* route detailed monetary history to [Advanced Learning](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/programs/advanced-modules/).

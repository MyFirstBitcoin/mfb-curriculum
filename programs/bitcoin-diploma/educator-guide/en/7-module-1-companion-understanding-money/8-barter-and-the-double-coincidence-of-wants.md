# 7.8 Barter and the double coincidence of wants

Use three people with mismatched goods and wants. First, let the group attempt direct exchange.


<!-- micrographic: barter-mismatch-01 -->
[Amina has rice and wants shoes, Luis has shoes and wants cooking oil, and Mei has cooking oil and wants repair work. No pair can trade directly, because neither person in a pair wants what the other offers.](/micrographics/barter-mismatch-01/en.md)


Then introduce a commonly accepted medium.


<!-- micrographic: sell-then-buy-01 -->
[Amina sells rice to one person for a commonly accepted money, then later uses that money to buy shoes from someone else. Selling and buying become separate actions.](/micrographics/sell-then-buy-01/en.md)


Ask:

* What made the first exchange difficult?
* Did [barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) fail because the goods had no [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value)?
* What changed when selling and buying became separate actions?
* Which problems remain even after money is introduced?

Clarify that [barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) can work in particular settings. The point is that it becomes difficult to coordinate at scale.

If using a game, debrief its simplifications. Real exchange includes relationships, credit, gifts, obligations, bargaining, law, and repeated interaction.

# 8.13 Sats and unit bias

Every learner should know:

> 1 bitcoin = 100,000,000 [sats](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi)

Use a few conversions:

* 0.1 bitcoin = 10,000,000 [sats](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi)
* 0.01 bitcoin = 1,000,000 [sats](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi)
* 0.001 bitcoin = 100,000 [sats](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi); and
* 1 [sat](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi) = 0.00000001 bitcoin.


<!-- micrographic: bitcoin-and-sats-01 -->
[A two-column table: 1 BTC is 100,000,000 sats, 0.1 BTC is 10,000,000 sats, 0.01 BTC is 1,000,000 sats, 0.001 BTC is 100,000 sats, and 0.00000001 BTC is 1 sat.](/micrographics/bitcoin-and-sats-01/en.md)


Ask why dividing one bitcoin into smaller units does not create more bitcoin. Compare cutting a measured quantity into smaller units without claiming the physical analogy explains the protocol.

Unit [bias](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bias) is the mistaken impression that a cheaper-looking whole unit is automatically better [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). Do not use current token [prices](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) as the teaching route.

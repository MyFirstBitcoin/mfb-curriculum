# 8.12 Finite supply and halving

Teach:

* new bitcoin enter through the allowed [block subsidy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-subsidy)
* the subsidy halves every 210,000 blocks
* the maximum approaches 21 million bitcoin
* [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) reject blocks that create more than the rules allow; and
* [transaction fees](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-fee) also compensate [miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner).


<!-- micrographic: bitcoin-issuance-schedule-01 -->
[The block subsidy starts at 50 bitcoin and halves every 210,000 blocks, while total issued bitcoin rises toward the 21 million limit. Transaction fees are paid on top and are not new bitcoin; no price forecast is implied.](/micrographics/bitcoin-issuance-schedule-01/en.md)


The limit is a public network rule, not a physical law. A person can write other software, but cannot force existing participants to adopt it. Incompatible adoption can create a different network rather than silently changing Bitcoin for everyone.

Do not connect a [halving](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving) or limited supply to a promised [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) increase. Supply is only one part of [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price); demand and market conditions matter.

#### Activity: issuance rounds

Use a fixed set of counters and release a decreasing number each round. Have node-role learners check whether the issued amount matches the rule card.

Debrief what the model misses: exact supply is software-enforced, [halvings](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving) occur at block heights, [miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) compete, fees exist, and counters do not reproduce Bitcoin's network.

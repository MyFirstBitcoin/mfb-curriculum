# 8.7 Explain the combined response

Use a five-part map:

1. A wallet uses a [private key](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) to authorize a transaction with a [digital signature](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature).
1. The transaction is [broadcast](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#broadcast) through a [peer-to-peer network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network).
1. [Miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) collect transactions and use [proof of work](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) to propose blocks.
1. [Nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) independently check transactions, blocks, [proof of work](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), and issuance rules.
1. Participants converge on the valid history with the most [accumulated work](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#accumulated-work).

No single part creates Bitcoin's credible [scarcity](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) alone. Avoid saying “the [blockchain](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain) solves everything.”

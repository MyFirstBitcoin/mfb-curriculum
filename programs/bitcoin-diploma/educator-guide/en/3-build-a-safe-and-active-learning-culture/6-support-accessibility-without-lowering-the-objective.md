# 3.6 Support accessibility without lowering the competency

For teaching and assessment, an accommodation may change how instructions are received, how a response is communicated, or how a device is controlled. It may include:

* larger print or screen magnification
* high-contrast or tactile materials
* additional processing time
* oral instead of written explanation
* written instead of spoken explanation
* keyboard, switch, voice, or assisted device control
* a reader or interpreter
* reduced noise or a private assessment space; or
* breaking a task into clearly signposted stages.

The accommodation must preserve the measurable competency. If the competency is to explain, the learner must still communicate the explanation. If it is to verify a [payment destination](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#payment-destination), the learner must still make or direct that verification. If it is to demonstrate a sequence, the educator must still observe the learner controlling the decisions in that sequence.

Accommodations for educator-led theoretical touchpoints or educator-observed practical evidence must preserve their purpose, competency, evidence, and standard. If an equivalent assessment cannot be established, provide further supported practice rather than certify the competency.

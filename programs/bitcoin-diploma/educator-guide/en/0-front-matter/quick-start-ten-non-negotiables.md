# Quick start: ten non-negotiables

These are guardrails, not a test. Keep this list open while you prepare. You do not need to memorize the curriculum before teaching; you do need to protect these boundaries every time.

1. Teach the four-module **Problem → Solution → Action → Connect** sequence: Problem—Understanding Money; Solution—How Bitcoin Works; Action—Using Bitcoin Safely; and Connect—Bitcoin in Your Community.
1. Treat **Modules 1–4** as the required Bitcoin Diploma. **Modules 1–2** develop theory-based competencies through explanation, comparison, and reasoning; **Module 3** develops safe practical action; and the educator-curated, required **Module 4** Connect stage links the shared learning to a local need, realistic alternative, safe task, and responsible next step.
1. Preserve the required competencies, evidence requirements, safety boundaries, and passing standard in every language and delivery mode.
1. Conduct competency-aligned educator-led theory interactions and direct practical observation. Keep any optional recap or independent review clearly separate from assessment evidence.
1. Give supported practice before assessment. For practical learning, use Presentation → Practice → Production.
1. Never ask to see, handle, photograph, copy, record, or store a learner's real [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase), [private key](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key), credential, balance, or unnecessary transaction information.
1. Use prepared wallets, simulations, test environments, printed screens, or appropriately low-stakes practice. Always prepare a no-funds or offline fallback.
1. Do not give financial, investment, legal, tax, accounting, compliance, or trading advice. Do not pressure learners to acquire or use bitcoin.
1. Treat named wallets, exchanges, services, maps, merchants, and community resources as dated local examples, not global recommendations.
1. Do not certify attendance, activity completion, an educator interaction without valid evidence, or either assessment component alone. Certification requires recorded theoretical and practical evidence.

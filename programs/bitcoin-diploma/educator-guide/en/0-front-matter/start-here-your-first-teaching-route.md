# Start here: your first teaching route

You do not need to be a Bitcoin expert or an experienced teacher to begin. You do need to prepare carefully, keep the learner safe, and be honest about what you know and what needs checking.

For your first delivery, use this route:

1. Read **Quick start** and **The educator's role**.
1. Read the relevant module guide only. You do not need to read the whole handbook before the first session.
1. Complete the module's **Prepare before class** list.
1. Choose one main activity and one backup activity, using the [Activity Library](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/activities/en/) for ideas when helpful. Do not try to use every option.
1. Copy the session competency and safety rule onto your board or opening slide.
1. Teach in the order **explain → ask → practise → debrief → check**.
1. Use the [Assessment Grid](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/vje9ehw2/staging/056d842fdf0602501c3ae7b5915277b0b7f857d4.pdf) and the operational assessment protocol when you are ready to collect evidence; ordinary discussion is not automatically assessment.
1. After class, write down what learners understood, what confused them, what needs checking, and what you will change next time.

If you have only ten minutes to prepare, read the module's purpose, learner promise, suggested flow, likely trouble spots, safety notes, and review point. That is enough to run a safe first session while you continue learning.

### What to read, and when


| When | Read first | Your immediate task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before your first course | Start here, Quick start, educator's role, safe practical teaching | Review the boundaries, choose the schedule, and prepare the room and materials. |
| Before Module 1 or 2 | The relevant module guide, facilitation methods, local verification record | Choose the opening question, one activity, one backup, and the local examples to check. |
| Before Module 3 | Safe practical teaching, Module 3 guide, practical evidence | Rehearse with a prepared or empty environment and define every stop condition. |
| Before Module 4 | Module 4 guide, Module 4 planning record, local expert briefing | Start with a local need and verify the selected task, tool, and current context. |
| Before using an activity for assessment | Assessment Grid and relevant assessment guidance | Explain the activity, prepare the learning environment, and decide what you will look for. |
| After each session | Review point, feedback and reassessment guidance, after-course reflection | Record what learners need next and update the following session. |


The rest of the handbook is a reference shelf. Read it when a learner asks a deeper question, when a local example needs checking, or when you are preparing a new delivery—not as compulsory pre-reading.

### What this guide assumes—and what it does not

It assumes that you care about learner safety and can follow a preparation checklist. It does not assume that you already know how to:

* explain technical ideas simply
* manage a discussion
* design an activity
* observe practical skill
* give useful feedback; or
* decide what evidence is enough.

Short scripts, question stems, activity instructions, examples, stop conditions, and record templates are provided for these tasks. Adapt the examples to your cohort, but do not improvise safety or certification standards.

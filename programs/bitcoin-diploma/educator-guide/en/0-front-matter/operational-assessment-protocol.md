# Operational assessment protocol

### A supportive assessment route

This protocol is the source of truth for the Bitcoin Diploma's seven assessed competencies and their required look-fors. Use it with the [Assessment Grid](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/vje9ehw2/staging/056d842fdf0602501c3ae7b5915277b0b7f857d4.pdf). Educator-evaluated theory is `C1`–`C5`; directly observed practice is `C6`–`C7`. Local language, examples, communication methods, activity choices, and delivery routes may change. Keep the competency and safety boundary clear.


| Competency | Required look-fors | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 Money and modern-money trade-offs | C1.1 Explain why people use money, barter limits, the beginner evolution of money, and its functions and properties. C1.2 Compare two forms of money using relevant characteristics. C1.3 Explain one modern-money trade-off through a local, observed, or realistic example without personal financial disclosure. | Individual explanation, comparison, and scenario response. |
| C2 Bitcoin foundations and rules | C2.1 Explain digital copying or double spending and how shared verification helps. C2.2 Demonstrate a beginner-level understanding of Bitcoin's rules and participant roles by explaining how shared rules are checked; what users, nodes, miners, and developers do; and proof of work, the 21 million supply limit, halving, and satoshis. C2.3 Explain how decentralization, finite supply, peer-to-peer transfer, and verifiability could help or limit Bitcoin's use as money, including “don't trust, verify.” | Individual explanation, explanation of participant roles, and an example of how a Bitcoin property could help or limit its use as money. |
| C3 Bitcoin and digital-asset comparison | Compare Bitcoin with another digital asset, such as a generic stablecoin, using relevant control, issuance, verification, custody, or counterparty criteria; state a trade-off and distinguish learning and responsible use from speculation or promotion. | One individual guided comparison; no numbered sub-points. |
| C4 Wallets, custody, and safety | C4.1 Explain wallets, addresses, keys, seed phrases, public versus secret information, and a custodial versus non-custodial trade-off. C4.2 Distinguish hot and hardware wallets, including why people use the latter, at beginner scope. C4.3 Recognize a scam, error, or high-risk request, including secret exposure, address mistakes, impersonation, or urgency, and explain when to stop, verify, or seek help. | Individual explanation and safety scenario. |
| C5 Payment and access pathways | C5.1 Compare on-chain and Lightning payments and choose a suitable beginner pathway in a guided example. C5.2 Compare access or exchange pathways and risks without recommending a provider or financial action. | Individual guided pathway comparison. |
| C6 Safe access and recovery readiness | C6.1 In an approved low-stakes or simulated setting, navigate a prepared wallet, complete or safely simulate a receive-or-send flow and supported first access or receipt, and verify the relevant destination, amount, network, and status. C6.2 Demonstrate recovery readiness using prepared or simulated materials while protecting all real secrets. | Direct observation of both distinct practical performances; one final result. |
| C7 Local Bitcoin-as-money task | C7.1 Safely perform one educator-selected locally relevant task or simulation, including required checks. C7.2 Explain its local purpose, realistic alternative, trade-offs, possible failure points, safety considerations, and realistic next step. | Direct observation of individual performance and explanation. |


Record **Showing competency: Yes** or **Showing competency: Not yet** for each parent competency. Every required look-for must be evidenced. A teaching activity may support several look-fors without becoming a separate test. `F1` (reasoned discussion) and `F2` (course reflection) are formative, not certificate gates.

* Record **Yes** when the learner has shown the competency sufficiently and safely. Record **Not yet** when more teaching, practice, or another opportunity is needed; this means the learner is building competency.
* Attendance, confidence, group membership, independent review results, and self-report do not replace a learner showing the competency. Activity completion can provide evidence when the activity is designed to reveal the relevant understanding or action.
* Use prepared wallets, simulations, testnet, physical tokens, or appropriately low-stakes arrangements. Learners should never be asked to risk significant funds or expose a [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase), [private key](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key), password, balance, or unnecessary personal information.
* When working as a group, make sure you can still notice each learner's relevant understanding, decisions, and safety behaviour. A friendly probing question is often enough.

### Planning time and cohort capacity

Assessment takes time, especially when learners need help completing an activity. Before the cohort begins, consider whether the educator or educators will have enough time to notice learner progress, answer questions, offer feedback, and reassess anyone who needs another opportunity.

Use a rough estimate if helpful: number of learners × likely support and observation time. This is planning guidance, not a fixed educator-to-learner ratio or an approval gate.

Allow time for setup, troubleshooting, feedback, accessibility accommodations, and reassessment. If learners need more time, extend the session, offer an after-class session, or return to the activity in a follow-up meeting. Adapt the route to the learners rather than rushing them.

Choose a practical route that feels safe and manageable for the learners. Record the selected Module 4 activity and any accessibility or low-tech adaptations if that will help you teach.

### Common support, safety, and record rules

**Permitted support:** Before evidence is gathered, you may read the prompt, repeat it once, provide an approved translation or communication accommodation, clarify a non-assessed word, and provide the prepared materials or environment. During practical work, you may give a safety stop instruction or reset a prepared simulation. You may not give the substantive answer, select the comparison criterion, identify the scam, tell the learner which button or payment detail to choose, complete a required practical step, or turn a group contribution into individual evidence without an individual response.

<!-- card: callout -->
> **Safety-critical practical errors**
>
> Record and pause the task if the learner reveals or attempts to share a [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase), [private key](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key), password, or unnecessary personal information; proceeds after a known address, invoice, amount, or recipient mismatch; accepts an obvious scam or urgent secret request; or bypasses a required verification step. Explain the reason kindly, reset to a safe environment, practise, and reassess later. The learner is building competency. Prepared training materials are the default; if an educator selects a live low-stakes activity, make its risks clear and provide a no-funds equivalent.


**Accessibility and low-tech equivalence:** Use the communication method, translated prompt, large-print or tactile material, oral response, described response, prepared paper wallet screens, role-play, or offline simulation that works for the learner while preserving the required action. An equivalent route should make the competency reachable; it should not reduce it to recognition, group participation, or self-report.

**Optional notes:** The [Assessment Grid](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/vje9ehw2/staging/056d842fdf0602501c3ae7b5915277b0b7f857d4.pdf) only requires one final **Showing competency: Yes** or **Showing competency: Not yet** result per competency and learner. Brief notes can help you remember what you saw, plan follow-up, or support a reassessment, but task IDs and lengthy evidence records are not required by the grid. Record only the minimum necessary learner information and keep it private.

### Example theoretical interactions

These interactions may be woven into normal module teaching. They are examples, not a single written exam or the only valid route. Give each learner a chance to respond, then record each competency separately.

**Understanding and action:** `T1`–`T3` check understanding: the educator listens as the learner explains or compares an idea. `P6a`, `P6b`, and `P7` check safe action: the educator watches the learner complete a practical task. These are suggested ways to gather evidence, not extra tests or grades. Record one final result for each competency in the [Assessment Grid](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/vje9ehw2/staging/056d842fdf0602501c3ae7b5915277b0b7f857d4.pdf).

#### `T1` — Money comparison and local scenario (`C1`)

**Prompt:** “Choose two familiar ways people hold or exchange value. Explain why people use money, why direct [barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) can be difficult, and how shared money changes that problem. Compare the two using at least two money functions or properties. Then choose one realistic money situation and explain one trade-off it creates.”

**Yes:** The learner gives a coherent beginner explanation, uses relevant comparison criteria rather than preference alone, and identifies a plausible trade-off without needing to disclose private finances.

**Not yet:** The learner only names terms, cannot explain the [barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) or money issue, makes an unsupported comparison, or cannot state a trade-off after permitted clarification. Provide support as the learner builds competency.

**Low-tech/equivalent route:** Use two printed picture or word cards and an oral, signed, scribed, or aided response.

#### `T2` — Bitcoin explanation, rule-and-role map, and asset comparison (`C2`, `C3`)

**Prompt:** “A digital unit is copied and sent to two people. Explain the problem and how shared verification helps. Use this map to explain peer-to-peer transfer, [decentralization](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization), users, [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node), [miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner), developers, [proof of work](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), public supply rules, the 21 million limit, [halving](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving), [satoshis](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi), and the beginner meaning of ‘don’t trust, verify.’ Then explain how a Bitcoin property could help or limit its use as money, noting a trade-off. Compare Bitcoin with a generic stablecoin or another crypto-asset using control, issuance, verification, [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), or counterparty. Why is learning this different from trading or promoting it?”

**Yes:** The learner accurately explains digital copying/[double spending](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending); identifies the required Bitcoin roles and relevant properties at beginner scope; explains how one Bitcoin property could help or limit its use as money without making an absolute claim; and makes a non-promotional comparison using relevant criteria.

**Not yet:** The learner materially confuses roles, claims Bitcoin guarantees [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), or universal acceptance, presents another [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) as identical without comparison, or cannot distinguish education from speculation/promotion.

**Low-tech/equivalent route:** Use a printed role-and-arrow map and comparison cards. The learner may point, arrange, sign, dictate, or explain through an approved communication aid.

#### `T3` — Custody, safety, and pathway decision (`C4`, `C5`)

**Prompt:** “A person receives a message asking for their recovery words, and also wants to receive a small payment. Explain what information may be shared and what must remain secret. Compare a custodial and non-custodial option, and explain the beginner difference between a hot wallet and a [hardware wallet](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hardware-wallet). What should the person do about the message, and when should they stop or seek help? Finally, choose an on-chain or Lightning path for a guided example and explain one relevant access or exchange trade-off.”

**Yes:** The learner distinguishes public receiving information from secrets, explains a [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) trade-off and hot/[hardware-wallet](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hardware-wallet) scope, identifies a safe stop/verify/help response and beginner practical limit, and makes a pathway comparison without making a provider or investment recommendation.

**Not yet:** The learner would share a secret, treats a wallet as holding bitcoin inside the device, proceeds with an obvious scam, or selects a pathway without relevant reasoning.

**Low-tech/equivalent route:** Use printed scenario cards and payment-request cards. Do not use a learner's real wallet, address, or financial information.

### Example practical routes

#### `P6a` — Supported first access and receipt (`C6`)

**Possible environments:** A prepared training wallet, testnet, no-value simulation, physical-token activity, educator-controlled empty setup, or an educator-approved low-stakes live setting. Personal funds are never required. Before selecting a live setting, explain its purpose and its privacy, security, and loss risks, and provide a no-funds equivalent.

**Learner task:**

1. Open or navigate the prepared environment and locate the correct receiving information.
1. Check the relevant address, invoice, amount, network, recipient, status, or [confirmation](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) detail before proceeding.
1. Complete or safely simulate a receive-or-send flow.
1. Complete the supported first-access pathway and receive bitcoin or a safe simulated receipt.
1. Check the resulting wallet or transaction information and state one safety boundary.

**Yes:** Completes the relevant steps safely, makes the important checks, protects secrets and [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), and gives an accurate brief explanation of the check made. The educator may adapt the sequence to the environment while preserving the competency.

**Permitted support:** Read the task, point to the prepared workspace, repeat a safety instruction, or reset a training environment. Do not indicate which payment detail is correct or complete a required step.

**Equivalent routes:** Printed wallet or payment screens with a transparent simulation; a role-play in which the learner selects and explains the relevant information; or a remote observation route that lets the educator see the learner's decisions without recording secrets.

#### `P6b` — Recovery-readiness simulation (`C6`)

**Learner task:** Using prepared recovery words or cards, or a disposable empty training wallet, explain which material must remain secret, arrange the recovery process in a safe order, identify one unsafe storage or sharing request, and state when to stop and seek help. Recovery words may be used for an empty disposable training wallet; they must never protect a learner's personal or funded wallet.

**Yes:** Protects recovery information, gives a plausible recovery-ready sequence, identifies an unsafe request, and never exposes a real secret.

**Permitted support:** Repeat instructions, provide accessible cards, or reset the prepared materials. Do not tell the learner which card or action is correct.

**Equivalent routes:** Large-print, tactile, oral, signed, scribed, or role-play sequence using fake materials only.

#### `P7` — Local Connect activity (`C7`)

**Choose a route:** Select a locally relevant activity such as a payment-path comparison, small-purchase simulation, work/remittance comparison, merchant/refund flow, community-donation simulation, pathway comparison, circular-economy/resource map, or another suitable equivalent. The activity should preserve the purpose of both `C7.1` and `C7.2`.

**Learner task:** Complete the variant's safe sequence and relevant verification behaviour. During or immediately after it, explain the local purpose, realistic alternative, relevant trade-offs and failure points, safety considerations, and a realistic next step. “This is not suitable here” is an acceptable conclusion when well reasoned.

**Yes:** Completes the activity safely and accurately explains the important local connections. The response is individual even if preparation was collaborative.

**Helpful support:** Read or explain the activity, clarify a non-assessed word, provide scenario materials, and stop unsafe action. Do not supply the comparison, risk, or next step that the learner is expected to explain.

**Equivalent routes:** Printed scenario and payment materials, local-language role-play, oral or aided explanation, or a simulation. A team presentation can support learning, but each learner still needs an individual opportunity to show the competency.

### Reassessment and optional quality support

**Reassessment:** **Not yet** means the learner is building competency and needs more learning, practice, or another opportunity—not “never.” Educators may return to reassess at any point, including during class, at a break, in an additional call, or in a follow-up session. Identify what the learner needs privately, provide focused practice, and use the same activity or a suitable equivalent. Do not lower the standard, use attendance as a substitute, or force the learner to disclose personal information. Update the [Assessment Grid](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/vje9ehw2/staging/056d842fdf0602501c3ae7b5915277b0b7f857d4.pdf) when the new evidence is sufficient.

**Optional educator support:** If educators are working together or feel uncertain, they can compare a **Yes** and **Not yet** example, discuss the standard, or complete the recommended Educator Training Program. This support can build confidence but is not a prerequisite for issuing certificates.

**Optional review:** A second educator can review a practical route, a difficult decision, or a reassessment when helpful. Review should support the educator's judgement, not replace direct observation or create extra paperwork for its own sake.

### Calibration examples for assessor discussion

Use these examples to compare judgements before delivery. They are model evidence, not learner scripts. Two assessors should score each example independently, discuss any difference, and record the date, assessors, selected `P7` variant, and agreed interpretation.


| Protocol | Annotated Yes example | Annotated No example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T2 — Bitcoin explanation, rule-and-role map, and asset comparison | The learner explains that digital information can be copied and that shared verification helps prevent the same bitcoin from being accepted twice. They correctly distinguish users, nodes, miners, and developers; describe the supply rule and halving at beginner level; compare Bitcoin with a generic stablecoin using control and issuance; and state one limitation without promoting either asset. Judgement: Yes. | The learner says miners can change any rule and that Bitcoin's fixed supply guarantees price. After permitted clarification, they cannot explain the copying problem or compare control and issuance. Judgement: No; practise roles, supply claims, and comparison criteria. |
| P6a — Supported first access and receipt | In a prepared simulation, the learner checks the recipient, network, amount, and payment detail before proceeding, completes the receipt, protects all secrets, and explains that a mismatch is a reason to stop and verify. Judgement: Yes. | The learner proceeds after being shown a recipient or amount mismatch, asks the educator which payment detail to choose, or exposes a recovery secret. Judgement: Not yet; stop, reset the safe environment, practise verification, and reassess. |
| P7 — Example variant: local payment-path comparison simulation | The learner completes the simulated payment sequence safely, checks the recipient and amount, and individually explains the local purpose, a realistic alternative, trade-offs, possible failure points, safety boundary, and next step. Judgement: Yes. | The learner completes the sequence only through educator direction, skips a required check, or cannot explain the realistic alternative and safety response after permitted support. Judgement: Not yet; provide focused practice and reassess the same or an equivalent variant. |


These examples do not replace direct observation, the [Assessment Grid](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/vje9ehw2/staging/056d842fdf0602501c3ae7b5915277b0b7f857d4.pdf), or the selected local `P7` approval. Do not record real secrets, private keys, passwords, balances, or unnecessary personal information during calibration.

Use the examples and activity materials that fit your cohort. Review local tools, current facts, translation, accessibility, and practical safety before delivery. When you adapt an activity, keep the competency and safety purpose clear.

# 4.4 Local Bitcoin tools and resources

Local bitcoiners may use wallets, exchanges, peer-to-peer markets, merchant tools, maps, meetups, hardware devices, and education resources. Availability and quality can change quickly.

Use this comparison before adopting a tool:


| Area | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | What problem does the tool solve? Is it necessary? |
| Custody | Who controls keys and funds? |
| Source | Who publishes it? Is the domain or application listing genuine? |
| Code | Is it open source? Is it actively reviewed and maintained? |
| Recovery | What happens if the device, service, or company disappears? |
| Privacy | What identity, transaction, contact, or device data are collected? |
| Cost | What fees, exchange spreads, subscriptions, or hardware costs apply? |
| Compatibility | Does it support the required wallet, network, address, or invoice? |
| Support | Is safe local help available without sharing secrets? |
| Law | Are there local restrictions, reporting duties, or tax effects? |


Named tools used in class are local examples, not endorsements by My First Bitcoin. Record the date when a tool was checked. A tool that was suitable last year may have different ownership, fees, features, or risks today.

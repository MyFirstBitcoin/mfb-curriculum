Complete this review individually, then compare your reasoning with your team or cohort. Consider the local example you studied: what real need did it address, which function of money was involved, and was [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) moved on-chain, through Lightning, or both? Ask who controlled the keys, what the strongest benefit and most serious risk were, and how local law, infrastructure, language, or culture affected the result. A responsible next step is one that fits the local context, makes costs and [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) clear, limits errors and scams, and can be evaluated against evidence.

**Module 4 answer:** Bitcoin can function as money when people voluntarily use it to earn, save, receive, send, spend, [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), or settle [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). The Connect stage begins with a real need, compares a realistic alternative, makes [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) and costs clear, respects local law and safety, and gives learners enough knowledge to choose freely.

#### Key takeaways

* Bitcoin as money is local: the useful pathway depends on community needs, infrastructure, laws, costs, tools, language, and safety conditions.
* An educator-curated **Module 4** Connect activity should link the Problem, Solution, and Action learning to a real problem or opportunity, realistic alternative, safe practice, and evidence of learning.
* Local workshops should use a safe sequence: observe the process, practice with support, then explain or complete a defined task without exposing secrets.
* Before using a local tool or service, check its purpose, [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) model, source, recovery process, [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), costs, compatibility, support, and current local rules.
* On-chain and Lightning payments make different [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) in settlement, speed, fees, [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), and failure points; confirm which payment method a recipient expects.
* Local exchange pathways, [KYC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#kyc) choices, merchant flows, and community use cases should be compared through their full costs, [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), privacy, safety, and legal [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off).
* A strong circular economy or community use case is voluntary, solves a real problem, protects participants, and does not make learners dependent on one service, organizer, or promise.

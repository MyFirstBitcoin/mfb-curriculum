# 4.2 Your local Module 4

In this module, you will apply an idea from **Modules 1–3** to a real need or opportunity where you live. You may explore earning, saving, receiving, spending, merchant use, community resources, or another local use of Bitcoin as money.

This matters because Bitcoin is experienced locally. You will better understand what is useful, what is not, and which conditions and risks shape a responsible choice. You will also become familiar with local Bitcoin tools, resources, and organizations without being expected to use or recommend them.

Your educator will guide you because they understand the local context. They may invite a local expert to help you learn from experience and interact positively and respectfully with your community. The goal is to build informed connections and help foster community, not simply to complete a [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction).

Your activity may explore questions such as:

* Can a local worker receive bitcoin for a service?
* Can a family compare a remittance path with an existing local method?
* Can a merchant accept payment without holding a large bitcoin balance?
* Can a community receive support while keeping [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) and records clear?
* Can you identify reliable Bitcoin support in the community?
* Can saving in [sats](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi) address a problem, and what new risk does it create?

A local example is not proof that the same method works everywhere. Explain what is specific to your community.

# 4.10 Circular economies

A [**Bitcoin circular economy**](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-circular-economy) is a network in which people earn, spend, save, and exchange bitcoin within a community. [Value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) circulates among workers, customers, merchants, producers, educators, and service providers instead of entering only through a one-time purchase and leaving through immediate conversion.

[In a Bitcoin circular economy, bitcoin passes from person to person in exchange for goods, services and time. A customer pays a merchant, the merchant pays a service provider, the service provider pays a worker, and the worker spends it again as a customer.](/micrographics/local-circular-economy-01/en.md)


My First Bitcoin supports [Bitcoin circular economies](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-circular-economy), and many are part of the Bitcoin Education Network. Explore these communities on the [Network Dashboard](https://network.myfirstbitcoin.org/?min10merchants=1).

A circular economy can reduce dependence on distant financial services and create direct local relationships. It can also fail if there is little demand, poor education, unreliable tools, weak security, or pressure to use bitcoin without a real need.

Map a simple local cycle:

1. Who can earn bitcoin?
1. Where can they spend or use it?
1. Which merchants can pay a supplier or worker in bitcoin?
1. Which participants need to exchange to or from fiat?
1. Who provides education and technical support?
1. What happens when internet access fails?
1. What [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) and [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) risks does each participant carry?

![](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/vje9ehw2/production/79370421e2d7dc74bd87d0f58ebe565a96a96d52-1536x1024.svg)

A healthy circular economy depends on voluntary participation. Merchants and workers should be able to identify the payment, pricing, [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), accounting, and [volatility](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#volatility) risks. Adoption numbers alone do not show whether the network is useful.

The strongest local loop solves a real problem for more than one participant. It should not depend permanently on giveaways or on one organizer processing every payment.

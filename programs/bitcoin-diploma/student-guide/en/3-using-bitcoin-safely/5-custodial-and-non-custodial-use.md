# 3.6 Custodial and non-custodial use

> **Definition – Custody**
>
> [Custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) answers a practical question: who controls the keys required to spend the bitcoin?


In a **custodial** service, a company or another person controls the keys. Your displayed balance is a promise from the [custodian](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian). You usually sign in with an account and ask the service to process a withdrawal or payment.

This difference is important for verification. Bitcoin's rules and total supply can be checked independently, but if you do not control the keys, you cannot independently check that the balance shown in your account corresponds to bitcoin the service holds and will make available to you. The public blockchain can show bitcoin controlled by keys; it cannot show that a service owes a particular customer the balance displayed in an account. Until you can withdraw and control the keys, your balance is the service's promise to provide bitcoin, not bitcoin you can directly verify and spend yourself.

In a **non-custodial** or **self-custodial** wallet, YOU control the keys. The wallet signs [transactions](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) under your authority. There may still be software, hardware, server, or network dependencies, but no [custodian](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian) must approve a valid on-chain spend.

[Custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) is not always obvious from the interface. If you cannot control the recovery method and need a company to approve a withdrawal, you are probably using a [custodian](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian). [Self-custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody) is not a test of personal worth; the important step is to identify the trust and risk involved.

**Try it:** Read two prepared wallet descriptions. For each, state who controls the keys, who could restore access, and one risk to check before using it.

# 3.12 Safety checklist

**Practice 3: spot a risk and stop safely**

What would make you stop before a payment or installation? Treat these as red flags: someone impersonates support or an educator; creates urgency or secrecy; promises guaranteed returns; asks you to send bitcoin first; changes a destination; or asks for keys, [recovery words](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase), remote access, or an advance fee.


<!-- micrographic: scam-red-flags-01 -->
[Five scam red flags: urgency, impersonation, a changed destination, guaranteed returns and requests for secrets.](/micrographics/scam-red-flags-01/en.md)


The safe response is always: **Pause. Share nothing. Send nothing. Close the channel. Verify independently through a known source. Ask for help without revealing secrets.** A known source is an official site, app listing, or contact method that you reached independently, not through an advertisement, direct message, or unexpected link. Check the publisher and web address, compare it with official documentation, and find contact details separately. If the source, instructions, or payment details differ from the demonstrated process, pause and seek help from your educator or trusted local support without revealing a [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase) or [private key](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key). A real opportunity can survive a careful check; a scam often depends on preventing one.

Protect recovery information: a [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase) can recreate a wallet's [private keys](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key). Keep it offline and out of view. Verify destinations by checking the payment method, recipient, address or invoice, amount, and final screen after copying or scanning. Use official instructions and a verified source for any wallet or service; be cautious with sponsored search results, direct-message links, and look-alike sites. Start with safe practice, a simulation, or a small approved amount; never borrow money to experiment.

**Try it:** In a prepared message or payment screen, identify the warning sign, what could be lost, and the first safe action.

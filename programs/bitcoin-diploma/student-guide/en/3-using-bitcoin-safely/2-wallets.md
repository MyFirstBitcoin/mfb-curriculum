# 3.2 Wallets

<!-- card: definition -->
> **Definition – Bitcoin wallet**
>
> A [Bitcoin wallet](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-wallet) is a tool that manages the information needed to receive and authorize Bitcoin [transactions](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction). It may be an application, a dedicated device, or a service. Bitcoin is recorded as [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) history on the shared [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger); a wallet manages the keys that control spending, rather than storing bitcoin on a phone or other device.


What would happen if your phone stopped working? The Bitcoin record would still exist, but access would depend on the keys and recovery method the wallet uses. A wallet helps create receiving information, show balances and [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) history, and authorize spending. It does not remove the need to understand who controls access.

<!-- card: dark -->
> **A wallet manages access; it does not hold bitcoin**
>
> Bitcoin is recorded on the shared [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger), not inside your phone. Before using a wallet, know who controls the keys, how access is recovered, and what would happen if the device or service failed.



<!-- micrographic: wallet-manages-keys-01 -->
[A wallet holds the keys. With them you can receive and send bitcoin, while the bitcoin itself stays recorded on the shared ledger, not inside the phone or device.](/micrographics/wallet-manages-keys-01/en.md)


The first question about any wallet is: **who controls the keys?** Before using a tool, check who can authorize a payment, how access is recovered, and which payment methods it supports. [**Open-source software**](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#open-source-software) publishes its code for anyone to inspect and review. This can improve transparency and make mistakes easier to find, but it is only one useful factor: it does not prove that a tool is safe, maintained, correctly installed, or suitable for your needs.

Compare [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), recovery, whether the code is publicly inspectable, compatibility, [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), cost, maintenance, and available support without treating any wallet as the global choice. Start with an educator's prepared environment; do not choose a tool because a stranger recommends it.


<!-- card: callout -->
> **Learning wallets and personal wallets**
>
> During the Diploma, you may create a wallet or write down [recovery words](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase) as part of practice. A classroom is a learning environment: information can be seen, copied, shared, or compromised by mistake. For this reason, a training wallet should normally be empty or simulated and should not be used to secure meaningful funds. Where practical, it is closed, reset, or returned at the end of the Diploma. Your educator may choose a low-stakes live activity, but should explain its purpose and the added privacy, security, and loss risks before anyone takes part. A wallet intended to secure meaningful funds needs a new, private setup and new recovery information. Your educator can explain the difference, but you are never required to set up a personal wallet to complete the Diploma.

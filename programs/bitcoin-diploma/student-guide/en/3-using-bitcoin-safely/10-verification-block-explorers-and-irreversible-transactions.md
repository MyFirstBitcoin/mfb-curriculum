# 3.11 Verification, block explorers, and irreversible transactions

> **Definition – Transaction ID and block explorer**
>
> A [transaction ID](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-id) ([_txid_](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-id)) identifies an on-chain [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction). A [block explorer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-explorer) shows public Bitcoin data, such as a [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction)'s status and [confirmation](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) count. It is a useful view, not an authority over Bitcoin and not proof of the recipient's identity or honesty.


What can this public information show, and what can it not show? It can show a [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction)'s status, destination, amount, fee, and [confirmation](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) count; it cannot prove why a payment happened or whether a person is trustworthy. An on-chain [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) is unconfirmed after it is [broadcast](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#broadcast) and gains its [first confirmation](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#first-confirmation) when included in a valid block. Later blocks add confidence to its place in the record, but they do not fix a wrong destination or a scam. There is no universal [confirmation](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) count for every payment; the suitable check depends on the amount and risk.

To inspect a prepared [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction), obtain its [_txid_](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-id), open a trusted explorer for the intended [Bitcoin network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network), and check the status, destination, amount, fee, and [confirmations](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation). An explorer is only an interface. Searching an address or [_txid_](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction-id) may reveal to its operator that your device is interested in that information. For practice, use an educator-prepared [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) or printed screenshot. Do not search a learner's personal address or [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) on a public service. If you later research a personal payment, learn the [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy) and security [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) of your connection before using a public service.


> **Callout – Public data is not proof of identity or honesty**
>
> A [block explorer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-explorer) can show public [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) data, but it cannot prove who controls an address, why a payment happened, or whether a recipient is trustworthy. Use prepared examples for practice and protect [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy) when searching public services.

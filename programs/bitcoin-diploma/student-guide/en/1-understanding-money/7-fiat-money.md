# 1.7 Fiat money

> **Definition – Fiat money**
>
> [Fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) is [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) issued under government authority, usually through a [central bank](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank), that is not redeemable for a fixed amount of a commodity such as [gold](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold). Governments support—and in some contexts impose—its use by requiring it for taxes and by setting legal-tender rules. The exact rules for accepting payments and settling [debts](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt) vary by jurisdiction.


[Fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) works because people, businesses, banks, and governments accept it. All money can develop [network effects](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#network-effect): people use it because other people use it and [prices](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) are stated in it. For [fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money), tax obligations, legal-tender rules, and institutional payment systems can reinforce that network.

> **Light – Examine a local payment**
>
> Choose one ordinary payment in your community, such as buying transport, food, or phone credit; receiving wages; or paying a bill. Use a realistic invented scenario if you prefer. In three or four sentences, explain:
>
> 1. what is being paid for and which [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) is used as the [unit of account](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#unit-of-account);
> 1. why the seller or recipient is willing to accept that [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency); and
> 1. which institutions, records, or rules people rely on to complete the payment.
>
> Finish by naming one way the payment would become harder if that shared acceptance or trust broke down.


Fiat systems can provide convenient payments, flexible credit, and tools for responding to financial crises. The same flexibility creates **risk**. A small number of institutions can change the supply and cost of money, restrict access, or make decisions that reduce [purchasing power](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power).

[Fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) requires layers of **trust**. Users trust banks to keep accurate records, payment services to process transfers, [central banks](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) to manage the [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency), and governments to maintain lawful institutions. These systems can work for long periods, but their rules are not fixed and can be open to neglect or abuse.

# 1.10 Why the US dollar is a world reserve currency?

A **reserve [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency)** is a [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) that [central banks](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) and governments hold and use for international payments. Before the First World War, the British pound played this role most strongly. The war weakened European [currencies](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) and financial centers, while the United States supplied goods and accumulated [gold](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold). This increased international use of the dollar.

After the Second World War, the United States had a large economy and much of its financial capacity intact. The 1944 [Bretton Woods](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bretton-woods) Agreement then organized a postwar system in which many [currencies](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) were linked to the dollar and the dollar was officially linked to [gold](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold), placing the dollar at the center of international payments.

In 1971, President Nixon ended the official exchange of dollars for [gold](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold) by foreign governments and [central banks](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank). This ended the dollar's official [gold](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold) [convertibility](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#convertibility) and brought the [Bretton Woods](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bretton-woods) system to an end. The dollar nevertheless remained widely used because of its established role in trade and finance, the size of the U.S. economy, and the depth of U.S. financial markets. It remains the leading reserve [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) today, although [central banks](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) also hold other [currencies](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) and its share can change over time.

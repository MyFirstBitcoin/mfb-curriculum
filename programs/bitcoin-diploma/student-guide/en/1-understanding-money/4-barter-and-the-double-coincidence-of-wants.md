# 1.4 Barter and the double coincidence of wants

> **Definition – Barter**
>
> [Barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) is the direct exchange of one good or service for another. It can work well in a small exchange, but becomes difficult when many people and products are involved.


Imagine that **Amina** has rice and wants shoes. **Luis** has shoes but wants cooking oil, not rice. **Mei** has cooking oil and wants repair work. Amina may need several exchanges before she can obtain the shoes. Each exchange takes time and requires agreement about [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value).

For [barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) to work directly, each person must want what the other person offers at the same time and in suitable amounts. Economists call this the [**double coincidence of wants**](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-coincidence-of-wants).

[Barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) also creates other problems:

* some goods cannot be divided without destroying their [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value)
* some goods spoil before they can be exchanged
* there is no common unit for comparing [prices](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price)
* saving large amounts can be difficult
* delayed payment and accounting become complex

A commonly accepted money separates selling from buying. Amina can sell rice to one person for money and later use that money to buy shoes from someone else.

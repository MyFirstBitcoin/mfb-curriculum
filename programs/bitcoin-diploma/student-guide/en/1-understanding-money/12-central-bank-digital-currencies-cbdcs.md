# 1.12 Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs)

> **Definition – Central bank digital currency (CBDC)**
>
> A [central bank digital currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc), or [CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc), is a digital form of [fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) issued as a [liability](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#liability) of a [central bank](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank): it is a claim that the [central bank](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) owes to its holder. It is different from physical cash and from a normal commercial-bank deposit, although the user experience may look similar.


[CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc) designs differ. Some are intended mainly for financial institutions; others are proposed for public use. First ask who the [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) is: with a [CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc), the [central bank](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) remains the [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) and can still apply monetary policy. A [CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc) therefore does not create a fixed monetary supply or remove central control; it simply changes the technology and distribution of [fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money).

Next ask what the design allows. Depending on the rules, a [CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc) may allow faster settlement, wider access, or new payment features. It may also give institutions greater ability to monitor [transactions](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction), apply rules, limit use, or connect identity directly to payments. This is centralized control: an institution, rather than ordinary users independently, sets or changes the relevant rules.

Digital does not automatically mean decentralized, scarce, private, or open to independent verification. A [CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc) may use digital technology, but it is still [fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) if its [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), supply rules, access rules, and payment permissions remain centrally managed.

When discussing a [CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc), ask:

* Who issues the money?
* Who can hold or use it?
* What information is collected?
* Can a payment be blocked, limited, or reversed?
* Can the rules change?
* Can ordinary users independently verify the rules?
* What happens without electricity or internet access?
* What legal protections exist?

Do not assume that every proposed [CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc) has the same design. Use current, local, and reliable information.

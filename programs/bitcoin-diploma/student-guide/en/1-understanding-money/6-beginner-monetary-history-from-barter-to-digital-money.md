# 1.6 Beginner monetary history from barter to digital money

Money did not develop in one identical sequence everywhere. Different societies used different goods, records, and institutions. However, a broad pattern helps explain modern money.

> “Money … is by no means the result of an agreement … but is the unintended product of historical development.”  
_Carl Menger, “On the Origin of Money” (1892)_

* **Direct exchange and local records:** People used [barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter), gifts, obligations, and written or remembered [debts](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt). These systems depended strongly on local relationships.
* **[Commodity money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#commodity-money):** Communities selected goods that were widely valued and easier to trade. Salt, beads, shells, livestock, copper, silver, and [gold](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold) have served monetary roles in different places. A commodity has a use or [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) beyond an [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer)'s promise.
* **[Standard coins](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#standard-coins):** Authorities measured and stamped metal into standard units. Coins improved recognition and accounting. Authorities could also reduce the precious-metal content of these coins while keeping the same stated [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). This is called [debasement](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debasement).
* **[Representative paper](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#representative-paper):** Transporting and protecting metal was difficult. Depositories and banks issued [paper claims](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#paper-claim) that could be redeemed for a stated amount of metal. The paper was easier to move, but users had to trust the [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) to keep enough metal and honor redemption. Under different versions of a [**gold standard**](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold-standard), a [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) unit was legally connected to a stated amount of [gold](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold). The rules varied by place and period, and governments or banks sometimes suspended or ended [convertibility](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#convertibility).
* **Bank and government money:** Banks issued more claims and credit. Governments created [central banks](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#central-bank) and national [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) systems. Money became increasingly based on institutional records and promises rather than direct possession of a commodity by the population. In many countries, national [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) later became no longer redeemable for a physical [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) such as [gold](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#gold). This separation from commodity backing is a defining feature of today’s [**fiat money**](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) (see below).
* **Digital money:** Much of today's money moves as digital records through banks and payment services. A card or phone sends an instruction; it does not contain money by itself. Usually, the instruction changes a [bank deposit](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bank-deposit), meaning the bank's recorded obligation—or claim—to pay the account holder an amount in the national [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency).

This history shows repeated [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off). Convenience increased, but users often became more dependent on monetary policy, [issuers](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), banks, and payment [intermediaries](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary).

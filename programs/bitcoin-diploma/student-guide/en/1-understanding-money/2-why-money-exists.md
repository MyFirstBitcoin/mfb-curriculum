# 1.2 Why money exists

People have different skills, resources, needs, and plans. Exchange allows them to specialize: a farmer can grow food, a builder can build homes, and a nurse can provide care. For this specialization to work at scale, each person needs a practical way to exchange the [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) of their work for the work of others.

Direct exchange is often difficult. The person who has what you need may not want what you offer. Money solves this coordination problem.


> **Definition – Money**
>
> Money is an [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) that people accept because they expect other people to accept it later. It helps strangers coordinate exchange, record [prices](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) and [debts](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt), and move [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) across time and distance.


Money helps strangers cooperate across time and distance, and at scale. For example, a worker can earn money today and spend it next year on a bicycle made in another country, paying for the work of many people they have never met. Money also creates a common way to record [prices](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), [debts](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#debt), income, costs, and savings.

Money is not the food, home, time, or skill that people [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value). It is a tool for measuring and exchanging claims on those things. Its usefulness depends on trust, rules, social acceptance, and its monetary properties.

# 1.13 Review: How can money break?

Pause and consider the money you use: what makes it useful in daily life, and which of its rules depend on institutions you must trust? [Barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) is difficult because matching needs is inefficient. [Fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) makes exchange easier, but its changing supply, institutional control, restricted access, and declining [purchasing power](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power) can create serious flaws for people who rely on it. People may disagree about how serious each flaw is or how to address it. This is why some people look for monetary alternatives with different [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) around supply, control, access, and trust.

#### Key takeaways

* Money helps people exchange, compare [prices](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), record [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value), and move [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) across time.
* The main functions of money are a [medium of exchange](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#medium-of-exchange), a [unit of account](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#unit-of-account), and a [store of value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#store-of-value).
* Monetary properties such as [durability](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#durability), [portability](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#portability), [divisibility](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#divisibility), [scarcity](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity), [acceptability](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#acceptability), and [verifiability](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verifiability) help us compare different forms of money.
* [Barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter) is difficult at scale because both people must want what the other offers at the same time and in suitable amounts.
* Money has developed in different ways across societies, often improving convenience while creating new [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) in trust, control, and access.
* [Fiat money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fiat-money) can support convenient payments and flexible credit, but its supply and rules depend on institutions.
* [Inflation](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#inflation), [fractional-reserve banking](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#fractional-reserve-banking), and [CBDC](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cbdc) design can affect [purchasing power](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power), access, [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), and the need to trust institutions.
* Some people look to alternatives such as precious metals when they want different [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) around [purchasing power](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#purchasing-power), supply, or control; every alternative has limits and risks.

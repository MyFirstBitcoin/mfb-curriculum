# How the Bitcoin Diploma works

### What learners will be able to explain and demonstrate by the end

After completing the Bitcoin Diploma, you should be able to:

* explain why people use money, compare two forms, and explain one realistic modern-money [trade-off](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) (`C1`)
* explain Bitcoin foundations and rules at a beginner level, including digital copying, shared [verification](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification), what users, [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node), [miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner), and developers do, how Bitcoin's supply is limited, and how a Bitcoin property could help or limit its use as money (`C2`)
* compare Bitcoin with another [digital asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) using relevant criteria and distinguish learning from speculation or promotion (`C3`)
* explain wallets, [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) choices, public and secret information, wallet types, and a safe response to a scam or error (`C4`)
* compare on-chain and Lightning payments and explain the risks of different access or exchange pathways (`C5`)
* safely complete supported wallet access and receipt with checks, then show recovery readiness using prepared or simulated materials (`C6`); and
* safely complete one educator-selected local Bitcoin-as-money task and explain its purpose, alternative, [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off), possible failure, and next step (`C7`).

These competencies are a foundation, not proof of expert skill. Completing the Diploma does not mean you are ready to manage large amounts of bitcoin, advise other people, or solve advanced technical problems without support.

### Flexible learning

Bitcoin Diploma content may be taught in person, online, through a blended course, or with print/PDF materials. The book provides the shared knowledge path. Your educator adds discussion, guided activities, demonstrations, local context, and any required assessment.

### Discussion and privacy expectations

Use discussion to test ideas, ask questions, listen carefully, and explain your reasoning. You do not need to agree with every claim or every person in your cohort.

During discussion:

* describe ideas in your own words
* ask for clarification when a term is unclear
* separate personal experience from general evidence
* challenge ideas without attacking people
* state uncertainty when you are unsure
* remember that financial access, security, and opportunity differ; do not assume that everyone shares your circumstances
* protect other people's private financial information
* treat personal stories shared in the cohort as private unless the person gives permission to repeat them
* do not photograph, record, or share another person's screen, work, or identifying information without permission
* do not pressure anyone to buy, sell, or reveal how much money they hold

Your educator will explain any required assessment activities. Theory is mainly developed in **Modules 1 and 2**, where your educator observes you explain, compare, justify, or apply an idea. A statement of confidence is not evidence by itself. Practical competency is mainly developed in **Modules 3 and 4**, where an educator observes the relevant actions under safe, supported conditions.

Your educator uses an assessment grid to record one result for each of the seven competencies above. `C1`–`C5` are assessed through your own explanations and decisions; `C6`–`C7` require direct observation. Both the wallet/receipt task and the recovery-readiness task are required parts of `C6`.

### Required prior knowledge: none

No prior knowledge is required. The course introduces each required term before using it in greater depth.

Basic reading and everyday arithmetic are useful. The most important calculation is that one bitcoin (BTC) can be divided into 100,000,000 smaller units called [satoshis](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi), usually shortened to [sats](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi). **BTC** is the common abbreviation used for amounts of bitcoin. A calculator may help, but advanced mathematics is not required.

If you already use Bitcoin, support your educator, share your experience, and explain ideas to peers in plain language. Teaching others is one way to consolidate your own learning. If you enjoy supporting learners, [contribute feedback](https://forms.myfirstbitcoin.org/feedback) or local community support, and explore the [Educator Training Program](https://etp.myfirstbitcoin.org/) to develop teaching practice.

### Required materials and technology expectations

For the Diploma, you need:

* this book in print or PDF form
* a notebook and pen, or another way to record answers
* an educator or study group where available
* time to discuss and reflect

A personal phone and continuous internet connection are not required for learning, practical preparation, assessment, or certification. Do not install a [Bitcoin wallet](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-wallet) or create a [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase) only because another learner tells you to. Use practical tools only under clear guidance and only when the purpose and risk are clear.

When it is time to practice with a [Bitcoin wallet](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-wallet), your educator will guide you step by step. You can learn without using your own money. These simple habits will help:

* use a device you control, and ask your educator if you need help choosing one
* ask your educator to help you check the download source before installing a wallet
* practice with a test environment, simulation, or other prepared training material; if your educator plans a low-stakes live activity, understand the purpose and risks before choosing whether to take part
* use sample [recovery words](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase) provided for practice, rather than words from someone else's real wallet
* keep real [recovery words](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase) (also called a [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase)) private: they restore access to a wallet, so do not show them to the class, photograph them, or enter them into a website or message

### Safety, responsibility, and no financial advice


> **Callout – Learn first; this is not a recommendation**
>
> The Bitcoin Diploma is for educational purposes only. It does not provide financial, investment, legal, or tax advice. It does not promise that bitcoin will increase in [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) or recommend that you buy, sell, borrow against, or hold any amount of bitcoin.
>
> The Diploma is Bitcoin-only. Other tokens, trading products, and broader cryptocurrency promotion are outside its scope. This boundary defines the subject of the course; it is not a guarantee about Bitcoin or a recommendation to acquire it.
>
> Bitcoin's market [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) can change quickly. [Transactions](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) can be difficult or impossible to reverse. A lost [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase), exposed [private key](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key), dishonest [custodian](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian), software error, or scam can cause permanent loss.


Use three rules throughout the course:

1. **Learn first.** Do not act only because of excitement, fear, or pressure.
1. **Start small.** Test unfamiliar tools and processes with an amount you can afford to lose.
1. **Don't Trust, Verify.** Check addresses, amounts, fees, sources, and people before you approve an action.

Responsibility also includes knowing when to ask for help. Seek help before moving meaningful funds, but never reveal a [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase) or [private key](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#private-key) to the person helping you.

### The four-module Diploma

The Diploma consists of a four-stage learning arc: **Problem → Solution → Action → Connect**. All four modules are part of the Diploma. **Modules 1 and 2** develop the shared Problem and Solution through explanation, comparison, and reasoning. Educators can adapt practical examples, tools, resources, and delivery in **Modules 3 and 4** while preserving the required safety and competency standards. **Module 4** is the educator-curated Connect stage, where learners link the shared learning to a local need or opportunity, a realistic alternative, safe practice, and a responsible next step.


<!-- micrographic: learning-path-roadmap-01 -->


**Module 4** is a required part of the Diploma, curated by the educator. Educators are the subject-matter experts on their students' local constraints, solutions, tools, resources, laws, language, infrastructure, and needs. The educator-curated Connect experience may include earning, saving, receiving, spending, [Layer 2](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#layer-2) solutions, local exchange pathways, merchants, or circular economies.

[Advanced Learning](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/programs/advanced-modules/) is outside the Diploma. It may examine deeper economics, [cryptography](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#cryptography), [mining](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) structure, Lightning operations, business use, or security. Advanced material is kept separate so the beginner Diploma remains clear and safe.

A topic is not unimportant because it is outside the Diploma. It may simply require more time or prior knowledge.

### How the learning path is organized

The four modules provide the required Problem → Solution → Action → Connect learning path, not a fixed class timetable. **Modules 1 and 2** develop theory-based competencies through explanation, comparison, and reasoning. **Module 3** develops safe practical action. **Module 4** is educator-curated but required: it connects that shared learning to a local need, realistic alternative, safe task, and responsible next step.


<!-- micrographic: four-module-table-01 -->


The Diploma takes at least eight hours. Four two-hour sessions are the shortest planned format, not a time limit. Your educator may divide a module across more sessions, revisit an earlier idea, or change the order of individual sections when your cohort needs more time for discussion, practice, review, or assessment. There is no upper time limit: the educator remains responsible for giving learners the time they need, covering all required competencies, and making the connections among the modules clear.

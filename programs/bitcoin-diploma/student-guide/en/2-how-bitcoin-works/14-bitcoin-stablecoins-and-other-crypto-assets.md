# 2.14 Bitcoin, stablecoins, and other crypto-assets

[Digital assets](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) can look similar on a screen while relying on very different systems. Bitcoin has public supply rules and no [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) that can create new bitcoin outside those rules. A stablecoin normally depends on an [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), reserves, legal claims, or a mechanism intended to track another [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency). Other crypto-assets may have different people or organizations controlling issuance, changing rules, or operating important parts of the system.

When comparing any [digital asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset), ask: Who controls issuance or changes? What can I verify independently? Who holds the keys? Which company, [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), or other counterparty must I trust? This Bitcoin-only Diploma uses the comparison only to clarify Bitcoin. It does not recommend acquiring, trading, or promoting any [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset).

The comparison below distinguishes categories and design choices. It is not an endorsement, recommendation, or a claim that any [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) is safe, reliable, or suitable for you.

> Scams, misleading claims, and unsafe services can affect any [digital asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) or payment pathway. Check the specific [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset), service, and [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) arrangement rather than assuming that a category is safe or unsafe by itself.


**Bitcoin (BTC)** is a [digital asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset) on the [Bitcoin network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network). Its [maximum supply](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#maximum-supply) and [issuance schedule](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuance-schedule) are public rules. People can hold bitcoin directly through keys and can independently verify Bitcoin rules by running suitable software. It can be sent globally without needing a central Bitcoin [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) to approve every [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction). Its market [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) can change sharply, and losing keys or recovery information can mean permanent loss; a mistaken payment is usually hard or impossible to reverse. Fees, [confirmation](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#confirmation) time, electricity, communications, public [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) records, and local acceptance can also affect use. A wallet provider or exchange can add its own counterparty and [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) risks.

Some projects use Bitcoin's code as a starting point but operate under different rules or networks. These are often called forks. A shared code history does not make a fork part of Bitcoin or give it Bitcoin's rules, participants, or security.

**Stablecoins**, such as USD Coin (USDC) and Tether (USDT), are digital tokens designed to track a reference [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value), often one unit of a national [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) such as the U.S. dollar. Some may be useful for moving a token [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) between compatible services or networks. Their design, reserves, redemption rights, and controls vary substantially. A stablecoin can lose its intended [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) (“depeg”), and users may depend on an [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer), reserve [custodian](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custodian), bank, legal redemption process, [blockchain](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain) operator, or bridge. [Issuers](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) or platforms may freeze tokens or restrict access where their rules or laws allow. Reserve quality, transparency, insolvency, regulation, smart-contract errors, scams, and network fees are additional risks.

**Other crypto-assets** are a broad category of [assets](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) used by other networks or applications. Their rules may support functions such as paying network fees, staking, governance, or application use. Issuance, control, technical design, and what can be independently verified differ from one [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) to another. [Prices](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) and liquidity can change rapidly, and developers, foundations, validators, administrators, or large holders may have significant influence, depending on the system. Rule changes, token issuance, smart-contract bugs, hacks, bridge failures, scams, platform failure, and unclear legal treatment can create loss or access risks. Holding a token does not automatically give a user ownership of a company, guaranteed income, or a right to redeem it for another [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset).


| Asset type | Main design | Key difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bitcoin | Public supply rules and no central issuer. | Its 21 million maximum is enforced by independently verified rules, not by reserves or redemption promises. |
| Stablecoins | A token designed to track a reference value. | The intended value depends on its particular rules, reserves, redemption process, and counterparties; these differ by stablecoin. |
| Other crypto-assets | Digital assets used on networks or applications. | Rules, issuance, and control vary by asset. |


Before using any [digital asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-asset), distinguish the [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset) itself from the service holding it for you. A familiar ticker, a stable-looking [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price), or a well-designed app does not remove the need to identify and compare [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody), counterparty, technical, legal, and loss risks.

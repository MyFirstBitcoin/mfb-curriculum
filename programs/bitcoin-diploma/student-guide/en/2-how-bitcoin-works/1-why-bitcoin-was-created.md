# 2.1 Why Bitcoin was created

Digital payments existed before Bitcoin. People could send money through banks, card networks, and online payment companies. These systems made commerce easier, but each depended on a trusted organization to keep the [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger), approve accounts, settle transfers, and resolve disputes.

This dependence creates power and risk. An [intermediary](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary) can delay a payment, charge a fee, collect and misuse personal data, restrict an account, reverse a transfer, or fail. The [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) of the [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency) can also change its supply.

For many years, computer scientists tried to create digital money that did not need a central [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) or payment company. The challenge was difficult. Digital information is easy to copy. If a digital coin could be copied and spent twice, it could not function as money. If one company prevented copying, the company became the point of control and failure.

Bitcoin was created to solve this problem with a [peer-to-peer network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network). Its design allows participants to agree on one [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) history without a central [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) keeper. It combines a public [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) ([blockchain](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#blockchain)), [digital signatures](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature), a network of [validating nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#full-node), [proof of work](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), and economic incentives.

Bitcoin does not remove trust from every part of life. It reduces the need to trust a central authority for the issuance and settlement of its native money.

In this book, **Bitcoin** with a capital B usually means the network, protocol, or wider system. And **bitcoin** with a lowercase b means the monetary unit or [asset](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#asset). **BTC** is the common abbreviation for an amount of bitcoin, especially when writing [prices](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) or specific quantities. A wallet or exchange is a service that may connect to Bitcoin; it is not the [Bitcoin network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network) itself.

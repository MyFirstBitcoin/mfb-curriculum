# 2.18 Review: How Does Bitcoin Address Monetary Flaws?

Pause and consider how public rules, finite issuance, peer-to-peer transfer, and independent [verification](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification) respond to the flaws identified in **Module 1**: changing supply, institutional control, restricted access, and reliance on trust. Why do [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) and [miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) have different roles, and why must [double spending](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending) be prevented? Bitcoin replaces some institutional trust with [verification](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification) and personal responsibility; it does not remove risk or [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off).

#### Key takeaways

* Bitcoin was created to make digital transfer possible without a central [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) keeper while preventing [double spending](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#double-spending).
* Its public [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger), [digital signatures](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature), [peer-to-peer network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network), [proof of work](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), and independent [verification](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification) work together to maintain a shared [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) history.
* Bitcoin is decentralized in the sense that no single participant can unilaterally change balances or the rules for everyone.
* Users choose whether to hold or accept bitcoin; [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) verify the rules; [miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) propose blocks; and developers can propose, but not impose, software changes.
* Bitcoin has a public supply schedule with a maximum of 21 million BTC, and [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) reject blocks that break this rule.
* One bitcoin (1 BTC) divides into 100,000,000 [sats](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi), so people can use smaller units without needing a whole bitcoin.
* Bitcoin can be evaluated as [sound money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#sound-money) using monetary properties, while still recognizing the [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off): [price volatility](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#volatility), limited acceptance, payments that are usually difficult or impossible to reverse, and the need for increased personal responsibility.

# 2.17 Empowerment, risk, and responsibility

Bitcoin's public rules, [peer-to-peer network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#peer-to-peer-network), and [digital signatures](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#digital-signature) give participants meaningful roles. Users can choose to **receive**, **hold**, and **send** bitcoin; [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) can independently verify the rules; [miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) can propose blocks; and developers can propose software changes. No single role controls the network for everyone.



These properties can empower participants, but they also place more responsibility on them. Without a central authority to approve [transactions](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction), recover access, or reverse many errors, each person must be able to identify the role and risks they accept.

![](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/vje9ehw2/staging/046836e98fde3a839b4969cfce7f63bfaf59b23f-1536x1024.svg)

For a person who chooses [self-custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody), control of the keys brings direct control of bitcoin. No company can freeze a correctly controlled on-chain balance. No company can restore a lost [seed phrase](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#recovery-phrase) either.

[Self-custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#self-custody) is therefore not just a technical feature. It requires protecting backups, verifying addresses, checking wallet behavior, and creating a recovery plan. The next module explains how to hold and use bitcoin carefully, including how to manage these risks and responsibilities.

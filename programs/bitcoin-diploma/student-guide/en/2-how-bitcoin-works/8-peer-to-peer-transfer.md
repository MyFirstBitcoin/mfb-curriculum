# 2.8 Peer-to-peer transfer

**Peer-to-peer** means that participants interact through a network without requiring a central coordinator. A Bitcoin user can create and sign a [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction), then [broadcast](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#broadcast) it to the network. The recipient does not need an account at the sender's bank because Bitcoin itself provides the common settlement system.

Peer-to-peer does not always mean that only two computers communicate directly. A [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) may pass through wallets, internet providers, and many network [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node). The important point is that no central Bitcoin operator must approve the sender or recipient.

Users can still choose [intermediaries](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary). An exchange may hold bitcoin for customers. A payment service may simplify an invoice. A hosted wallet may manage keys. These services can be convenient, but the user then depends on the service's rules and security.

Bitcoin makes direct ownership and transfer possible; it does not force every user to avoid every service. The [custody](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#custody) choice determines who has practical control.

Peer-to-peer transfer can be useful when people are in different places or use different banking systems. It also creates responsibility. A valid [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) sent to the wrong address cannot usually be reversed.

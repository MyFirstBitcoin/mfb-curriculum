# 2.16 Bitcoin origin timeline

Bitcoin appeared through a short series of public events.


<!-- micrographic: bitcoin-origin-timeline-01 -->


Explore the source records: read the [Bitcoin whitepaper](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-whitepaper) ([source PDF](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf)), inspect the [Genesis Block](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#genesis-block) ([block record](https://mempool.space/block/000000000019d6689c085ae165831e934ff763ae46a2a6c172b3f1b60a8ce26f)) and the first recorded [person-to-person transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#person-to-person-transaction) ([transaction record](https://mempool.space/tx/f4184fc596403b9d638783cf57adfe4c75c605f6356fbc91338530e9831e9e16)), and read the [original pizza request](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=137.0). A [block explorer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#block-explorer) is a convenient interface to public data, not a primary authority over Bitcoin's rules.

[**Satoshi Nakamoto**](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-nakamoto) is the pseudonym used by Bitcoin's unknown creator or creators. [Satoshi](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-nakamoto) communicated online, released the software as open source, and later stopped participating.

No central company owns Bitcoin. The software and rules continued because other people chose to run, review, and develop them. This origin matters: the creator did not remain as a permanent leader who could issue more coins or approve [transactions](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction).

The [Genesis Block](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#genesis-block) and the 2008 financial crisis give Bitcoin important historical context. However, evaluate Bitcoin by its design and operation, not only by a story about its creator.

In October 2008, [Satoshi Nakamoto](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#satoshi-nakamoto) announced the [Bitcoin whitepaper](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-whitepaper). Its central question was simple: **how can people make online payments without letting the same digital unit be spent twice or putting one [intermediary](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#intermediary) in control?**

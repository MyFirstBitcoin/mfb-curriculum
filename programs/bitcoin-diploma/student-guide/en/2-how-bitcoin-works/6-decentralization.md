# 2.6 Decentralization

A [centralized system](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#centralized-system) has a controlling point. A bank controls its customer [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger). A payment company controls its network. A government controls the issuance rules of its [currency](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#currency).


> **Definition – Decentralization**
>
> A [decentralized system](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralized-system) distributes roles and decision enforcement among many independent participants. Bitcoin has no central office that can create accounts, change balances, or approve membership.


Bitcoin's [decentralization](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization) comes from several features:

* anyone can download and inspect the [open-source software](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#open-source-software)
* users can transact without opening an account with the [Bitcoin network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network)
* independent [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) check [transactions](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) and blocks
* [miners](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) compete to propose blocks
* no [miner](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#miner) can make an invalid block acceptable to honest [nodes](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node)
* changes to the rules require voluntary adoption by participants
* the [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) is copied across many computers and locations

[Decentralization](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization) is not the same as equal influence. [Mining](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work) equipment, technical knowledge, money, internet access, and software development are not distributed equally. Large service providers can become points of dependence for their own users. Bitcoin's claim is narrower: no single participant has the authority to unilaterally change the [ledger](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#ledger) or monetary rules for everyone.

[Decentralization](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization) also creates costs. Coordination can be slower. Users must verify information and take more responsibility. There is no central support desk for the [Bitcoin network](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#bitcoin-network). These [trade-offs](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#trade-off) are part of the design, not accidental defects that can be removed without changing the system.

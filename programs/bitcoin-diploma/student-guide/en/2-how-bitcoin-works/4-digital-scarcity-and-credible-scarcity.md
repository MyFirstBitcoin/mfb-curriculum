# 2.4 Digital scarcity and independently verifiable supply

Digital information is normally easy to copy.


<!-- card: definition -->
> **Definition – Digital scarcity**
>
> Digital [scarcity](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) means that a system can distinguish a valid unit from an invalid copy and prevent the same unit from being spent twice.


Bitcoin creates digital [scarcity](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) by combining signatures, a public [transaction](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#transaction) history, [proof of work](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work), and independent rule [verification](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification). A bitcoin can be transferred, but the network rejects an attempt to spend the same bitcoin again.

[Scarcity](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity) is independently verifiable when people can check the public rules for themselves and no single [issuer](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#issuer) can quietly change the supply for everyone. This is why Bitcoin's supply limit must be studied together with [decentralization](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#decentralization) and [node](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#node) [verification](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#verification).

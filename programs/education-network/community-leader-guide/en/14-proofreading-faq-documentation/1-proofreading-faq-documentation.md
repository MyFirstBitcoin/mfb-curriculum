# 3.1 Translating and Proofreading FAQ/Documentation

Our programs are open-source Bitcoin education, written so that any educator can teach with them. Translating them is one of the most direct ways to help: every new language means educators can teach Bitcoin in the language their students think in. This chapter explains why the work matters, what you can expect, and exactly how to do it.

**In this chapter:**

1. Why we translate
1. Translation, now open to anyone
1. Before you start
1. Roles
1. Step by step: getting started, translating, proofreading, and when your language is complete

## 1. Why we translate

My First Bitcoin exists to empower independent educators worldwide with open-source Bitcoin education. An educator can only teach with material their students can read. A translated program opens the material to every educator and learner who speaks that language.

In other words, when you translate or proofread a program, you are not just helping yourself or your students. You are giving every educator in your language a complete, ready-to-teach program, thus empowering those who empower others.

## 2. Translation, now open to anyone

Our materials have always been free to translate, adapt and use, with no permission needed. But in practice, few people could. Translating a program meant much more than translating its words:

* You had to lay out the translated text yourself, often in professional design software.
* You had to produce and publish the finished PDF.
* Coordinating several people on one language was hard, so most translations depended on one person with the time, the skills and the resources to do everything.

That has changed. We now provide the infrastructure:

* **Professional translation tools.** You work in Crowdin, the same kind of platform professional translation teams use, from your browser.
* **Automated design and publishing.** Once a translation is approved, our workflow produces the designed PDF and publishes it on our website. You never touch a layout.
* **Coordination built in.** Any number of people can work on the same language at the same time, each on the parts they choose.

So the only things you need are a language you know, some time, and an internet connection. No design skills, no software to buy, no publishing to organise. Translating Bitcoin education is now open to anyone who speaks a language.

## 3. Before you start

Please read these before you accept an invitation:

* **Credit goes to your community.** Your work earns points for your community on the [Leaderboard](https://leaderboard.myfirstbitcoin.org/transparency), not an individual score.
* **A shout-out for everyone.** When a language is published, we thank its contributors together on social media, without ranking anyone by role or by how much they did.
* **The materials stay open source.** You do not gain ownership of the material, and your name does not appear in the program content itself.
* **Releases come as translations do.** Approved translations go live roughly once a month, as they are completed. There is no fixed schedule, because it depends on when translations come in.

## 4. Roles

There are two roles:

* **Translator.** Translates the text into your language, improves existing translations, and votes on other people's suggestions.
* **Proofreader.** Can do everything a translator can, and also approves the final version of each line. Where translators prefer different styles, the proofreader for that language decides.

**Style guides.** Any translator or proofreader can ask for a style guide for their language: a short document with your language's conventions (tone, formality, how to handle names and Bitcoin terms). The proofreader writes it and sends it to us, and we add it to Crowdin, where everyone translating that language sees it.

## 5. Step by step

##### 5.1. Getting started

1. Sign up at [forms.myfirstbitcoin.org/proofread](https://forms.myfirstbitcoin.org/proofread). Tell us your language and whether you want to join as a translator or a proofreader.
1. We invite you to Crowdin for your language. Crowdin sends you an email invitation.
1. Accept the invitation. If you do not have a Crowdin account yet, create one; it is free.
1. Open the My First Bitcoin project in Crowdin and choose your language. You will see the programs available to translate.

##### 5.2. Translating

**How to use the Crowdin platform: [Crowdin's detailed guide to using its online editor](https://support.crowdin.com/online-editor/)**. Optional reading: the editor is intuitive to use, so treat this as reference material to consult when you need it.

1. **Open a file.** Choose a program and open it in the Editor. The original text is on one side, and your language's translation field on the other.
1. **Decide how to work.** Most texts already have an AI-made first draft. The work is yours, so choose what suits you:
  * **Keep it** when the draft is right. You can vote for it.
  * **Improve it:** edit the draft in the translation field and save it as your suggestion.
  * **Start from scratch:** clear the field, type your own translation and save it.
1. **Vote.** Use the plus and minus buttons on other people's suggestions. The best-rated suggestion rises to the top, which helps the proofreader.
1. **Use the glossary.** Bitcoin terms that have an agreed translation are highlighted in the text. Hover over them to see the agreed term. Crowdin warns you if your translation uses a different term.
1. **Ask when unsure.** Leave a comment on the line. Other translators and the proofreader can answer there.

##### 5.3. Proofreading

**How to use the Crowdin platform: [Crowdin's detailed guide to using its online editor](https://support.crowdin.com/online-editor/)**. Optional reading: the editor is intuitive to use, so treat this as reference material to consult when you need it.

1. **Open a file** in the Editor, in your language.
1. **Show what needs review.** Filter the lines to those not yet approved.
1. **Review each line** against the original. Check meaning first, then terminology (the glossary), then style.
1. **Approve.** Click the approve button on the best suggestion. If none is right, edit it, save, and approve your own version. You can also select several lines and approve them together.
1. **Settle style questions** for your language, and write a style guide if translators need one (see Roles).

##### 5.4. When your language is complete

Once every line of a program is approved in your language:

1. We check the translation, archive it, and record your community's credit.
1. Our workflow produces the designed PDF and publishes the program on our website.
1. We announce the new language on social media and thank everyone who helped.

## 6. Questions

**Do I need to translate a whole program?** No. Work on as much or as little as you like. Many people working on one language is exactly what the tools are for.

**Can I ignore the AI draft?** Yes. Keep it, improve it or replace it.

**What if I disagree with another translator?** Vote, and leave a comment on the line. The proofreader for your language makes the final call.

**When will my translation be published?** When all of a program's lines in your language are approved, it goes into the next release. Releases come roughly monthly, as translations come in.

**Will my name be on the program?** No. Your contribution is credited to your community on the Leaderboard and thanked publicly when the language is published.

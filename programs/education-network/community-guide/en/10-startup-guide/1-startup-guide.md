# 10.1 Startup Guide

_Written for communities setting up in the United States. Most steps work anywhere: swap the U.S. forms and agencies for your country's own._

This guide helps you turn your Community into a working nonprofit: a legal entity, a bank account, a small team, and the documents that partners and funders will ask for.

Other chapters of this guide already cover teaching, materials, visibility, meetups, Geyser and measuring impact, so this chapter does not repeat them. Where one of those chapters is the better place, we point to it.

The most important thing is to be biased toward action. Start with step 1, and do not wait until everything is perfect.

This guide is a living document. Feedback and suggestions are welcome: write to arnold@myfirstbitcoin.org.

## 1. Become a nonprofit: 501(c)(3) status

* Check that your organisation is eligible for tax-exempt status.
* Gather the documents you need, including your mission statement and financial details.
* Expected time: 4 to 5 weeks.
* **Action:** file [Form 1023 or Form 1023-EZ](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1023-ez) with the IRS, with the fee.

You do not need a legal entity to start teaching or to raise your first funds on Geyser (see _How to Set up Your Geyser_). Most grant facilities, however, require 501(c)(3) status or an equivalent.

## 2. Banking: Bitcoin and traditional

* **Bitcoin:** choose a Bitcoin or Lightning service to receive donations. We use [Zaprite](https://zaprite.com/); [Alby](https://getalby.com/) is another option.
* **Traditional:** open a dedicated nonprofit checking account at a bank or credit union that is friendly to Bitcoin organisations. We use [Mercury](https://mercury.com/).
* Expected time: 1 day to 1 week.

## 3. Know the rules for nonprofits

* Learn the local and federal regulations that apply to nonprofits, and the basics of nonprofit financial management.
* **Resource:** the IRS's free [workshop for small and mid-size tax-exempt organisations](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/stay-exempt/virtual-small-to-mid-size-tax-exempt-organization-workshop).

For what to teach and how, see _Learning Materials_ and the Educator Training Program.

## 4. Build your team

* Define the key roles, and the skills each one needs.
* A good first team member is an educator committed to learning and sharing the Bitcoin Diploma.
* A good second team member is a generalist who knows how to operate. We look for mission alignment first, then systems thinking, digital literacy, clear written and verbal communication, and the ability to follow through.
* Our best contributors have found us. When we do recruit, we use [Bitcoiner Jobs](https://bitcoinerjobs.com/).
* **Action:** write each role down with a scope of work, KPIs and how you will evaluate it.
* **Templates:** our [Scope of Work guide](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1bIvr1O4CLWTD3a9maBNMmnNxZa9pBhgA) and the [Team Evaluations Tracker](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1QG1XWsiwGibNFY8SViOMJCcUolL7xK2-/view).

## 5. Draw your organisational chart

* Show your structure, who reports to whom, and the key responsibilities.
* **Action:** start from one of [Miro's organisational chart templates](https://miro.com/templates/organizational-charts/).
* See also our [Accountability Chart guide](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1W1CykeFk6Ykir_OJQFlfP8MiQqiEaeW0): one owner for every responsibility.

## 6. Your one-pager

* Summarise your mission, goals and work on a single page, with a clear call to action.
* **Action:** once your Community reaches a reputation score of 100 on the [Education Network Leaderboard](https://leaderboard.myfirstbitcoin.org/), you can get a free one-pager generated from your live leaderboard data. See _Set up your Profile in Leaderboard_.

## 7. Your pitch deck

* Build a short, clear presentation of your purpose, strategy and results, with real stories and your plans.
* Keep it short: [a good pitch can be under 3 minutes](https://x.com/callmehouck/status/1617877513832894468).
* **Action:** outline your pitch in a document first, then design the deck.

## 8. Sponsorship proposals and follow-up

* Write a proposal that says clearly how funds will be used and what difference they make.
* Keep a simple follow-up system for sponsors and donors. A spreadsheet is fine to start; move to a CRM as your list grows.
* **Template:** a simple [Fundraising Dashboard](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1yAmhZD_MQ1clNGABGS-6P5jsnzcUA9fr/view) to start with.

For which funding to accept, which to refuse, and the grant facilities aligned with our work, see _Funding & Sustainability_ (unlocks at a reputation score of 500).

## 9. Stay compliant

* Review and update your legal documents regularly.
* **Template:** a [Non-Disclosure Agreement](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1XuUVvp5KP8-vnORnlsVFpt_aBWkWalIl/view) for advisory board members. Have it checked for your country.
* Keep good records, and review them with your CPA or tax professional.
* Compliance rules are regional. If you find a lawyer in your region who understands Bitcoin, tell us so we can share it with other Communities.

## 10. Set goals and adapt

* Set quarterly and monthly goals, and review them.
* Use feedback and data to decide what to change.
* **Resource:** see _Measuring Impact_ for objectives and key results (OKRs) and a weekly scorecard, and My First Bitcoin's [Operating System](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1uleXQj3yIZbSsMRC2uit-pXHVt90W3NU) (ten steps, from an annual vision plan to standard operating procedures).

For how to measure and report what your Community does, see _Measuring Impact_.

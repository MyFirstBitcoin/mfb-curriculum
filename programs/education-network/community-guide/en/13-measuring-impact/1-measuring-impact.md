# 12.1 Measuring Impact

Measuring impact helps you see what works, decide what to do next, and show partners and funders what your Community achieves. Measure what matters, keep it simple, and be honest about it.

In the Education Network, impact is about people empowered: the educators you train and support, and the communities you help grow. A headline count of students reached says less than how many educators keep teaching, and how many communities keep growing.

This chapter follows the operating system My First Bitcoin uses itself: an annual vision, objectives and key results (OKRs), and a weekly scorecard. The full [Operating System](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1uleXQj3yIZbSsMRC2uit-pXHVt90W3NU) (ten steps, from the annual vision plan to standard operating procedures) is in our Open Source Everything folder.

## 1. Start with an annual vision

Everything you measure should connect to where you want to be in a year. Write a short annual vision plan with your team, and review it every quarter.

* Look back: what did you achieve last year, and what was hard?
* Look at today: where does your Community stand, and what opportunities or threats do you see?
* Decide: your mission, a few ambitious but realistic goals, and the areas you will focus on.
* Write it down: objectives, the resources you need, a timeline with milestones, and who is responsible for what.
* Share it with everyone in your team, so each person sees how their work connects to it.

**Include:** mission statement, core values, annual objectives, key metrics, resources needed, timeline, and what success looks like.

## 2. Set objectives and key results (OKRs)

OKRs turn your vision into clear goals for each person or role.

* **Objectives** are qualitative: what you want to achieve, in an inspiring sentence. For example: "Build a strong team of educators."
* **Key results** are measurable: how you will know you got there. For example: "Train and onboard 5 new educators by the end of Q3."
* Use 3 objectives per role, each with 3 key results. Fewer is better than too many.
* Set them from the top down: the leads first, then the team, so each level aligns with the one above.
* Make each person write their own, so they own them.

### Review them

* **Monthly:** a quick status per key result: on track, lagging, or off track.
* **Quarterly:** review the OKRs, adjust them, and set the next quarter's.
* Talk about progress and blockers in your one-to-ones.

## 3. Keep a weekly scorecard

A scorecard is the simplest way to stay on track: one number per role, updated every week.

* Pick one number per role that should grow over time and is directly tied to that role's impact.
* It must be easy to measure every week, clear, and unambiguous.
* Update it weekly and look at it in your team meeting.
* Watch the trend, not one week. Act quickly when it drops, and celebrate when it grows.
* Use it for coaching, not punishment. Change the number if it turns out not to matter.

## 4. What to measure

Choose what fits your goals. Some examples for a Community:

* Active educators, and new educators trained
* Cohorts started and completed, and graduates
* Meetups and events held
* Materials translated or proofread
* Local businesses and organisations reached
* Feedback from students and participants

Count only what you can show. A smaller number you can prove is worth more than a big estimate.

## 5. Show your proof of work

Your reputation score on the [Education Network Leaderboard](https://leaderboard.myfirstbitcoin.org) is how the network sees your Community's work. Report your activities on the [reputation report form](https://forms.myfirstbitcoin.org/report-reputation) as they happen: it is your public, verifiable track record, and it unlocks the next chapters of this guide.

## 6. Share your impact

Publish a short, regular update: what you did, what changed, and what you learned. Pair it with a brief financial overview (see _Funding & Sustainability_). Funding tends to follow proof of work, and transparency builds trust.

**Action:** write down 3 objectives for the next quarter, each with 3 key results, and pick one weekly number to track.

# 4.1 How to Organize a Meetup

A monthly meetup is the simplest way to grow a local Bitcoin community. It brings together people who are curious, people who are learning and people who already use Bitcoin, in one room. This chapter is a checklist you can follow from your first meetup on. For a larger, full-day event, see _How to Run an Unconference_.

## 1. Set your goal

* Keep it simple: help people and local businesses understand and use Bitcoin.
* Decide who you want in the room: newcomers, students, local businesses, existing Bitcoiners, or a mix.

## 2. Choose a date and a venue

* Pick a day and time that suit your community, then keep the same rhythm (for example, the last Thursday of every month). Consistency builds attendance.
* Choose a venue with room for everyone, ideally one that accepts Bitcoin. If it does not yet, the meetup is a good chance to show the owner the benefits.
* Ask the venue to promote the meetup to their own customers.

## 3. Promote it

* Announce the date, time and place on your social channels, and keep repeating it.
* Make a simple flyer, digital and printed. Use your profile and visuals from _Visibility & Reach_.
* Use a registration page (for example Meetup.com, Eventbrite or the Orange Pill App) so you know who is coming.
* Share information in every language your community speaks.

## 4. Support, not sponsorship

* Help in kind from the venue (space, a discount for Bitcoin payments) is welcome.
* Be careful with sponsors who want visibility or a say in what is presented. A meetup is for education, not product pitches. See _Funding & Sustainability_ for when to say no.

## 5. On the day

* Arrive early and test the screen, sound and internet.
* Welcome people at the door and introduce newcomers to each other.
* Keep the content practical: Bitcoin basics, why it matters, and how to use it safely. Use the _Learning Materials_.
* Include local businesses: how accepting Bitcoin can help them.
* Create a safe, welcoming space where every question is a good question.

## 6. After the meetup

* Ask attendees for short feedback.
* Write down what worked and what to change, and update your checklist.
* Keep in touch with attendees and the venue between meetups.
* Share photos (with permission) and a short recap on your channels.
* **Report it:** add the meetup on the [reputation report form](https://forms.myfirstbitcoin.org/report-reputation) (choose "Organized a meetup" and add a link to the event). Each meetup counts toward your Community's reputation score.

**Action:** pick the date of your first meetup, and announce it this week.

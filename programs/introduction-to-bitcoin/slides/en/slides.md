Money is a tool for exchange.

## Money Is a Tool for Exchange

Nobody wants money for its own sake. It is useful because others accept it, expecting others to accept it later.

![](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/vje9ehw2/staging/11c8ef017ba2a817db5029a4902751e0814b4558-1774x887.svg)

<!-- notes: A banknote is just a piece of paper. Money solves the barter problem by separating selling from buying: you sell your rice to one person and buy shoes from another. This is what separates money from barter. -->

---

![bg right:40%](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/vje9ehw2/staging/608140649b513680f20281ddc9a3b2f28b0f8883-1054x1493.png)

## Three Things Money Does

* **Medium of exchange:** people accept it in payment
* **Unit of account:** prices are expressed in it
* **Store of value:** it moves value from today into the future

No money stores value perfectly, but some forms lose purchasing power more slowly than others.

---

![bg right:40%](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/vje9ehw2/staging/a9efb037f62e6bfc928c9192ce4e5178d285dc26-1942x809.svg)

## From Shells to Paper

* Shells, salt, beads, livestock, copper, silver, gold
* Precious metals: scarce, durable, widely accepted
* Paper claims redeemable for a stated amount of metal
* Gold standards: a currency unit legally linked to gold

The rules varied by place and period.

---

## Fiat Money

* 1971: the United States ended the official exchange of dollars for gold by foreign governments and central banks
* Today most national currencies are fiat money: issued under government authority, not redeemable for gold or any other commodity
* _Fiat_ is Latin for "let it be done", often understood as "by decree"
* Accepted because people, businesses, banks, and governments use it; laws make it legal tender and require it for taxes

# What's Wrong With Our Money?

When people hear the word inflation, they usually think of prices going up.

---

## Inflation and Purchasing Power

<!-- micrographic: same-money-smaller-basket-01 -->
[The same 100 units of currency buy the full basket of basic goods when it costs 100, but not when the same basket costs 110. The price level has risen and the purchasing power of the money has fallen.](/micrographics/same-money-smaller-basket-01/en.md)

<!-- notes: Price inflation is a sustained rise in the general level of prices. Its other side is purchasing power: how much a unit of money can buy. If a basket of basic goods rises from 100 units of currency to 110, the same 100 units no longer buy the full basket. -->

## Inflation and Purchasing Power

## Many Causes, One Choice

* Expansion of money and credit
* Supply disruptions and changes in demand
* A weakening currency, taxes, production costs

One cause is a choice, not an accident: in a fiat system, a small number of institutions can increase the supply of money and credit and change its rules.

<!-- notes: Economists debate how much each cause matters. Keep the focus on the one cause that is a decision rather than an accident. -->

---

## Scarcity and Value

* The original Mona Lisa is worth more than any copy: it is the only one
* Scarcity alone does not create value, but when people want something, its supply matters
* When the supply of money grows faster than what it can buy, each unit tends to buy less

---

## Inflation Does Not Affect Everyone Equally

* Cash and savings may lose purchasing power
* Owners of assets that rise in price may be partly protected
* Wages and prices adjust at different speeds

A bank balance is different from cash in your hand: it is the bank's promise to pay you. That promise usually holds, but it depends on the bank and on the rules around it.

---

## Try It: A Purchasing-Power Check

1. Choose one ordinary item: bread, transport, or phone credit
1. Write down its price today
1. Ask someone what it cost several years ago, and check a reliable record if you can
1. Did incomes rise at the same rate? What else might explain the change?

<!-- notes: This works without any device or internet. Compare answers across the group, and remind learners that one price is not the whole inflation measure. -->

---

## Fiat Money Works, With Risks

* Convenient payments, flexible credit, tools for responding to crises
* But its supply and rules can change, and the people who use it have little say


<!-- card: callout -->
> This is why people look for alternatives, and why Bitcoin was created.

# What is Bitcoin?

Digital money that is decentralized, has a limited supply, and can be sent peer to peer without a bank's permission.

---

## The Double-Spend Problem

**31 October 2008:** Satoshi Nakamoto publishes _Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System_. **January 2009:** the network starts.


<!-- micrographic: central-ledger-vs-bitcoin-01 -->
[With a payment company, one organization keeps the ledger and approves each transfer. With Bitcoin, the sender signs a transaction, independent nodes check it and miners add it to one shared ledger, so the receiver depends less on a central authority.](/micrographics/central-ledger-vs-bitcoin-01/en.md)

<!-- notes: Before Bitcoin, sending money online always depended on a trusted organization: a bank or payment company that kept the ledger, approved accounts, and could delay, block, or reverse a payment. Digital information is easy to copy: if a digital coin could be spent twice, it could not work as money. This is the double-spend problem. Satoshi Nakamoto is a person or group using that name. -->

---

## Decentralized · Limited Supply · Peer to Peer

* **Decentralized:** no central office creates accounts, changes balances, or approves who joins
* **Limited supply:** there will never be more than 21 million bitcoin
* **Peer to peer:** send to anyone, anywhere, without asking a central authority

No company can freeze bitcoin that you control yourself. The other side of that freedom is responsibility: a valid transaction is usually impossible to reverse, and there is no help desk for the network.

<!-- notes: Decentralized does not mean everyone has equal influence. It means no single participant can change the ledger or the monetary rules for everyone. -->

---

## A Limit Anyone Can Check

<!-- micrographic: claimed-or-verified-supply-01 -->
[A supply limit written on a website or in an issuer's database can be changed quietly by that issuer. Bitcoin's supply rule is public, and signatures, a public history, proof of work and node verification together stop new coins from being invented or the same bitcoin from being spent twice.](/micrographics/claimed-or-verified-supply-01/en.md)

<!-- notes: New bitcoin are released on a public schedule, and independent computers called nodes reject any block that breaks that rule. The limit is credible because anyone can check it. It is not a law of nature: it holds because participants keep enforcing it. A limited supply does not guarantee that the price will rise; price depends on demand as well as supply. -->

---

## You Don't Need a Whole Bitcoin

One bitcoin divides into 100,000,000 smaller units called satoshis, or sats.


<!-- micrographic: bitcoin-and-sats-01 -->
[A two-column table: 1 BTC is 100,000,000 sats, 0.1 BTC is 10,000,000 sats, 0.01 BTC is 1,000,000 sats, 0.001 BTC is 100,000 sats, and 0.00000001 BTC is 1 sat.](/micrographics/bitcoin-and-sats-01/en.md)

<!-- notes: Owning 100,000 sats and owning 0.001 bitcoin describe the same amount. Address the misconception that Bitcoin is out of reach because one bitcoin is expensive. -->

---

## Bitcoin Has Trade-Offs

* Its price can change quickly
* Mistakes are usually permanent
* Acceptance is still limited in many places
* Using it safely takes some learning

Bitcoin replaces some trust in institutions with verification and personal responsibility. It does not remove risk.

# How Do You Use Bitcoin?

The first question about any wallet: who controls the keys?

---

## A Wallet Manages Keys

<!-- micrographic: wallet-manages-keys-01 -->
[A wallet holds the keys. With them you can receive and send bitcoin, while the bitcoin itself stays recorded on the shared ledger, not inside the phone or device.](/micrographics/wallet-manages-keys-01/en.md)

<!-- notes: A Bitcoin wallet is a tool, usually an app, that lets you receive, check, and send bitcoin. Your bitcoin is not stored inside your phone: it is recorded on the blockchain. The wallet manages the keys that let you prove it is yours and authorize a payment. -->

---

## Who Actually Holds Your Bitcoin?

* **Self-custodial:** you control the keys
* Nobody can recover your bitcoin for you if you lose the seed phrase and the device
* **Custodial:** a company holds the keys, a bit like a bank
* Your balance is its promise to pay; it can limit withdrawals, freeze an account, be hacked, or shut down

Choosing custody is not a test of worth. What matters is knowing which kind you are using and what you are trusting.

---

![bg right:40%](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/vje9ehw2/staging/8e2d6e494c02d0a8b9deae4a9cd45594ae2052c1-1536x1024.svg)

## Your Seed Phrase Is Full Access

An ordered list of words, often 12 or 24, that can recreate your keys. Anyone who has it can take your bitcoin.


<!-- micrographic: seed-phrase-on-paper-01 -->
[A seed phrase is an ordered list of words, here twelve numbered sample words written on paper. The right words in the right order can restore control in a compatible wallet, so it stays secret and offline.](/micrographics/seed-phrase-on-paper-01/en.md)

<!-- notes: The seed phrase is not only a backup: it is full access. Write it down accurately on paper when you create the wallet; keep it offline, private, and somewhere it will not be lost or damaged. Never photograph it, email it, store it in cloud notes, send it in a message, or type it into a website. The words shown are a sample only; never show or collect a real seed phrase. -->

## Before You Use Any Wallet

* **Keys:** who controls them, you or a company?
* **Recovery:** what if your phone is lost or the company disappears?
* **Source:** official website or app store listing, not a link someone sent you?
* **Code:** open source and actively maintained? (Not proof of safety on its own.)
* **Costs:** fees for sending, receiving, or withdrawing?
* **Payments:** on-chain, Lightning, or both?

<!-- notes: My First Bitcoin does not recommend specific wallets. Tools, fees, and owners change, and what suits one community may not suit another. Point learners to a trusted local educator. -->

---

## On-Chain and Lightning

* **On-chain:** recorded directly in the blockchain
* **Lightning:** a payment network built on top of Bitcoin for fast, small, frequent payments, such as buying a coffee
* A Lightning wallet can also be custodial or self-custodial


<!-- micrographic: on-chain-and-lightning-01 -->
[On-chain payments are recorded directly in Bitcoin's blockchain, while Lightning is a payment network built on Bitcoin for fast, frequent payments that can also fail. Either path can be custodial or self-custodial.](/micrographics/on-chain-and-lightning-01/en.md)

---

## Three Rules While You Learn

* **Learn first.** Do not act because of excitement, fear, or pressure.
* **Start small.** Try unfamiliar tools with an amount you can afford to lose.
* **Don't Trust, Verify.** Check the person, the payment method, the address, and the amount.


<!-- card: callout -->
> **Share receiving information, never secrets**
>
> An address or a payment request can be shared. A seed phrase or private key must never be shared. No legitimate educator, wallet company, or support worker will ever ask for it.

## Stop, Share Nothing, Send Nothing

Stop if someone creates urgency or secrecy, promises guaranteed returns, asks you to send bitcoin first, or asks for your seed phrase. Pause. Share nothing. Send nothing. Then check through a source you already know.


<!-- micrographic: scam-red-flags-01 -->
[Five scam red flags: urgency, impersonation, a changed destination, guaranteed returns and requests for secrets.](/micrographics/scam-red-flags-01/en.md)

# What's Next?

This is just the beginning.

## Four Takeaways

* **Money depends on rules and trust.** Fiat supply and rules are set by a small number of institutions.
* **Bitcoin offers different rules.** A public, limited supply, open to anyone, that can be checked rather than trusted.
* **Safety comes first.** Learn first, start small, verify everything, never share your seed phrase.
* **This is just the beginning.** One safe next step is enough.

# What is Money?

**Money is a tool for exchange.**

Nobody wants money for its own sake. We want it for what it can buy. A banknote is just a piece of paper. What makes it useful is that other people will accept it in exchange for things we actually want, because they expect others to accept it later.

This is what separates money from barter.

In a barter system, trading only works when both people want what the other has, at the same time and in suitable amounts. Economists call this the **double coincidence of wants**. If you grow rice and need shoes, the shoemaker may want cooking oil, not rice. Money solves this by separating selling from buying: you sell your rice to one person and buy shoes from another.

**Money usually does three things.**

It is a **medium of exchange**: people accept it in payment. It is a **unit of account**: prices are expressed in it, so we can compare the value of different things. And it is a **store of value**: it moves value from today into the future. No money stores value perfectly, but some forms lose purchasing power more slowly than others.

Throughout history, many things have served as money: shells, salt, beads, livestock, copper, silver, and gold. Precious metals became widely used because they were scarce, durable, and widely accepted. But carrying and protecting metal is difficult, so banks began issuing paper claims that could be redeemed for a stated amount of metal. Under different versions of a **gold standard**, a currency unit was legally linked to an amount of gold. The rules varied by place and period.

Over time, money came to rest more on institutions and promises than on metal. In 1971, the United States ended the official exchange of dollars for gold by foreign governments and central banks. Today, most national currencies are **fiat money**: currency issued under government authority, usually through a central bank, that cannot be redeemed for a fixed amount of gold or any other commodity. The word fiat is Latin for "let it be done", often understood as "by decree".

Fiat money works because people, businesses, banks, and governments accept it. Laws usually make it legal tender and require it for paying taxes, and people want it because everyone around them uses it.

**We went from money backed by something, to money backed by trust in someone.**

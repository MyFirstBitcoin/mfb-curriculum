# How Do You Use Bitcoin?

A **Bitcoin wallet** is a tool, usually an app, that lets you receive, check, and send bitcoin. Your bitcoin is not stored inside your phone: it is recorded on the blockchain. The wallet manages the **keys** that let you prove it is yours and authorize a payment.

So the first question about any wallet is: **who controls the keys?**

###### Who actually holds your bitcoin?

![](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/vje9ehw2/staging/f93df0cc4a2ad82b3410a9ad4c3273d1b760f8ea-1774x887.svg)

With a **self-custodial wallet**, you control the keys.

When you set it up, it shows you a **seed phrase** (also called a recovery phrase): an ordered list of words, often 12 or 24. It can recreate your keys on another device. Anyone who has it can take your bitcoin. The seed phrase is not only a backup: it is full access.

Protect it:

* write it down accurately on paper when you create the wallet;
* keep it offline, private, and somewhere it will not be lost or damaged;
* never photograph it, email it, store it in cloud notes, or send it in a message;
* never type it into a website.

If you lose your seed phrase and your device, nobody can recover your bitcoin for you.

With a **custodial wallet**, a company holds the keys for you, a bit like a bank. Your balance is the company's promise to pay you. Setup is familiar and support may be available, but you depend on that company: it can limit withdrawals, freeze an account, be hacked, or shut down.


| Question | Custodial | Self-custodial |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who holds the keys? | The company | You |
| Who handles recovery? | The company, through your account | You, through your seed phrase |
| Main trade-off | Convenience and support, but you depend on the company | Direct control, but you are responsible for backups and mistakes |


A useful phrase in the Bitcoin world: **not your keys, not your coins.** Choosing custody is not a test of worth. What matters is knowing which kind you are using and what you are trusting.

My First Bitcoin does not recommend specific wallets. Tools, fees, and owners change, and what suits one community may not suit another. Ask a trusted educator what people use locally, and check these points yourself before using any wallet:

* **Keys:** who controls the keys, you or a company?
* **Recovery:** what happens if your phone is lost or the company disappears?
* **Source:** did you download it from the official website or app store listing, not from a link someone sent you?
* **Code:** is it open source and actively maintained? (Open source alone does not prove a tool is safe.)
* **Costs:** what fees apply to sending, receiving, or withdrawing?
* **Payments:** does it support the payments you need, on-chain or Lightning?

Bitcoin has two common ways to pay. **On-chain** payments are recorded directly in the blockchain. **Lightning** is a payment network built on top of Bitcoin for fast, small, frequent payments, such as buying a coffee. A Lightning wallet can also be custodial or self-custodial.

Three rules will keep you safe while you learn:

1. **Learn first.** Do not act because of excitement, fear, or pressure.
1. **Start small.** Try unfamiliar tools with an amount you can afford to lose.
1. **Don't Trust, Verify.** Check the person, the payment method, the address, and the amount before you approve a payment.

**Share receiving information, never secrets.** An address or a payment request can be shared. A seed phrase or private key must never be shared. No legitimate educator, wallet company, or support worker will ever ask for it.

Stop if someone creates urgency or secrecy, promises guaranteed returns, asks you to send bitcoin first, or asks for your seed phrase. **Pause. Share nothing. Send nothing.** Then check through a source you already know.

# 4.3 Wire Tap

_Simulation, Game, Discussion, Experiential · Whole class, Small groups · 40–50 min_

Students explore how information changes depending on who carries it and who is allowed to hear it. Through a series of short message-passing games with changing rules, learners experience trust, mistakes, and intentional interference firsthand. Reflection focuses on how messages can change, leak, or be protected, and who should decide who gets access to information.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Experience how information can change when passed between people
* Recognize the difference between mistakes and intentional changes
* Understand that not everyone should hear every message
* Begin thinking critically about trust, [privacy](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#privacy), and control of information

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “See what happens to a message when different people carry it.”

#### Materials

* Prepared short and long message cards (for teacher reference) (See Appendix A)
* Paper and pencil for running dictation variant
* Open classroom space for movement
* Whiteboard or poster board for reflection notes
* Markers for reflection notes
* Nice-to-have: Simple symbol or substitution code sheet for optional locked message (See Appendix B)
* Nice-to-have: Extra printed message cards—one posted copy per team—and tape or clips for the running-dictation variant
* Nice-to-have: Timer

#### Setup

* Prepare one simple and one complex message, then decide teams of 6–8 students.
* Clear a safe space for students to line up and move.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**5 minutes**

1. Ask whether students have played telephone and whether the message always stays the same. Explain that they will test who can be trusted with a message; avoid technical or abstract terms.

##### Step 2: Activity

**30-35 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Divide the class into small teams. Explain that they will run several short rounds with the same structure but different rules, and that no one is in trouble for mistakes.

###### Model

1. Demonstrate the whispering rules with a very short example: whisper only, with no repeating or fixing mistakes.

###### Carry Out

###### Round 1 – Easy Message (Trusted Chain)

1. Whisper a very simple sentence to the first student; students pass it down the line, and the last student says it aloud.
1. Checkpoint: Was the message correct? Did anyone change it on purpose? Did it feel easy to trust the group?

###### Round 2 – Hard Message (Complex Information)

1. Repeat the whisper chain with a longer, more precise sentence, then have the last student say it aloud.
1. Checkpoint: What changed? Where did it break? Was anyone being bad, or was it just hard?

###### Round 3 – Hidden Rule (Intentional Change)

1. Use the same complex message, but secretly tell one student in the middle that they may change one word. Do not announce the rule; then run the whisper chain.
1. Checkpoint: Did this feel different from mistakes? How could we tell something was wrong? What felt unfair?

###### Optional Variant – Running Dictation (Control vs Trust)

###### Round 4 – Trusted Runner

1. Place the written message across the room. One student runs to read it, returns to tell the writer, and the writer records it.
1. Checkpoint: Was this clearer than whispering and why?

###### Round 5 – Untrusted Space

1. Add a listener near the runner’s path who may hear, but not see, the message.
1. Checkpoint: Who learned something they shouldn’t have and was the message still safe?

###### Optional Final Twist – Locked Message

1. Repeat running dictation with the message written in a simple symbol code (_See Appendix B_).
1. Ask students to observe who can understand it.
1. Checkpoint: Who can read it and who hears it but learns nothing?

###### Reflection

1. Ask who should hear everything, who should hear nothing, and who should decide; allow students to name the problem in their own words.

##### Step 3: Wrap up

**5 minutes**

1. Thank students for participating honestly, reset the room, and reinforce: “Being careful with information helps protect people.”

#### Key points

* Observe how messages can change in transit and explain how trust affects privacy and accuracy.

#### Discussion questions

**5-10 minutes**

1. Review that messages can change, leak, or be altered.
1. Reinforce that trust depends on rules and boundaries.
1. Connect to earlier lessons on public versus private information.

#### Notes

###### Classroom management

* Normalize mistakes and emotional reactions while maintaining calm control.
* Do not accuse or reveal the hidden rule-breaker unless students explicitly ask.

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Write a class rule for protecting messages or redesign the game to make it fairer.

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: shorter messages and fewer rounds.
* ELL/Accessibility: visual cues, slower pacing, buddy support.
* Safety: controlled movement and clear whispering rules.

###### Suggested assignment

At home, pass a short message through two people and compare the final message with the first one. Tell a parent or caregiver what can happen when messages travel.

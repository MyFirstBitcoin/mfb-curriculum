# 4.5 Nostr

_Simulation, Game, Craft · Small groups · 50–60 min_

Building on earlier activities about trust, message passing, and interception, students now compare open information transfer with encoded communication. Learners first experience how relaying information requires trusting intermediaries and how messages can be overheard or altered. They then introduce cipher wheels to protect messages, discovering that information can remain private even when passed through untrusted or public channels. ### Pre-requisites

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Review how information transmission requires trust in intermediaries
* Recognize how messages can leak or change when transmitted openly
* Understand that encoding protects information even when intermediaries are untrusted
* Successfully encode and decode vocabulary using a cipher wheel.

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “Find a better way to send messages without everyone needing to be trusted.”

#### Materials

* Prepared open-message cards for transmission (at least one per team)
* Cipher wheel templates for each student (See Appendix A)
* Worksheets or blank paper for transcription
* Pencils or markers
* Scissors
* Split pins or brads for cipher wheels
* Open classroom space for movement
* Two labelled tables or workstations per team: sender and receiver
* Nice-to-have: Example simple and complex messages (See Appendix B)
* Nice-to-have: Role cards for Sender, Runner, and Receiver; a score chart or stickers for points
* Nice-to-have: Timer

#### Setup

* Set two tables far apart for each team and place open-message cards on each sender table.
* Prepare cipher-wheel materials, but keep them hidden until Phase 2.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**5 minutes**

1. Ask students to recall the previous whispered or relayed-message activity and the problems they noticed with trust, mistakes, or overhearing. Remind them that messages could not stay private, then explain that they will test whether that problem can be solved.

##### Step 2: Activity

**40–45 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Divide students into teams of three and assign fixed roles: Player 1 is the Sender, Player 2 the Runner, and Player 3 the Receiver.

###### Model

1. Demonstrate a short running-dictation round with an open message. Point out that the runner must repeat it exactly and that anyone nearby can overhear it.

###### Phase 1 – Open Transmission (Trust Required)

1. Player 1 silently reads the message, then quietly reads it to Player 2. Player 2 relays it verbally to Player 3, who writes it down; Player 2 may run back and forth as needed. The first team to transmit it successfully wins.

###### Checkpoint Discussion

* Ask whether the final message matched the original.
* Ask whether runners overheard other teams.
* Ask whether the runner had the power to change the message.
* Reinforce that the system only worked if the runner was trusted.

###### Phase 2 – Encoded Transmission (Trust Reduced)

1. Distribute and assemble cipher wheels. The Sender and Receiver privately agree on a letter shift; Player 1 encodes a new message, Player 2 relays only the encoded text without knowing its meaning, and Player 3 transcribes and decodes it.

###### Competition Rules

* Teams earn points for correct decoding.
* Speed and accuracy both matter.
* Runners are not allowed to learn the cipher.

###### Reflection

1. Ask what changed between open and encoded transmission, whether the runner still needed to be trusted, and who controlled access to the message in each phase.

##### Step 3: Wrap up

**5 minutes**

1. Collect shared materials and reset the room, while letting students keep their cipher wheels. Reinforce: “You don’t need to trust everyone when your message is protected.”

#### Key points

* Use encoding to explore safer message transmission without trusting intermediaries.

#### Discussion questions

**5-10 minutes**

1. Review that earlier activities showed how messages can be intercepted or altered.
1. Review that encoding allows messages to travel through public space safely.
1. Connect this to the idea of choosing what information is public or private.

###### Classroom management

* Older students can choose their role in the relay
* Control running paths and enforce walking if needed.
* Emphasize that mistakes are expected and part of learning.

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Rotate roles and repeat with a new cipher.
* Increase message length or add decoy messages.

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: single words and fixed letter shift.
* ELL/Accessibility: alphabet charts and slower pacing.
* Safety: clear movement rules and supervised tool use.

###### Suggested assignment

Explain to a parent or caregiver how a code can help keep a message private, even when it travels through a public place.

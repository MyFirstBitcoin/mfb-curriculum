# 4.4 Cypherpunks Write Code

_Craft, Experiential · Whole class, Individual · 45–60 min_

Students are introduced to the idea of codes as a way to hide and protect information. Through a hands-on craft, learners build a simple cipher wheel and use it to encode and decode Bitcoin-related vocabulary. The activity combines fine motor skills, logical thinking, and early cryptographic concepts while reinforcing new terminology in a playful and concrete way.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Understand the meaning of encode and decode; recognize that changing a code changes the output; use a cipher wheel to encode and decode simple words; correctly decode and encode selected Bitcoin vocabulary.

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “Make a secret code tool and use it to hide and read words.”

#### Materials

* Printed cipher wheel templates with inner and outer alphabet rings (See Appendix A)
* Printed worksheet with encoded Bitcoin vocabulary (See Appendix B)
* Pencils, crayons, or markers
* Scissors
* One brad or split pin per cipher wheel
* Whiteboard or poster board
* Whiteboard or poster markers
* Nice-to-have: Pre-made cipher wheel for demo, extra practice worksheets
* Nice-to-have: Projector demonstration, Left-handed scissors

#### Setup

* Print the cipher-wheel templates and worksheets; prepare one large wheel or board version to demonstrate.
* Set out scissors and pins safely.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**5 minutes**

1. Ask students what a secret code is and where they have seen one, such as in games, secret messages, or locks. Introduce _encode_ and _decode_ in simple language, and explain that they will use a code to hide and reveal words.

##### Step 2: Activity

**35-45 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Explain that shifting letters makes secret messages; draw the alphabet and show a simple shift, such as A becoming D.

###### Model

1. Demonstrate encoding and then decoding a short word with a letter shift. Introduce the cipher wheel and show that both its position and the number of turns change the output letters.

###### Carry Out

###### Part 1 – Cipher Wheel Craft

1. Students cut out the inner and outer alphabet circles, place the smaller circle on the larger one, and fasten them with a pin so the wheel turns freely. They then test it by aligning different letters.

###### Checkpoint

1. Decode one word together, checking that students understand how changing the wheel changes the result.

###### Part 2 – Encode and Decode Practice

1. Students use their cipher wheel to decode worksheet words and encode Bitcoin vocabulary words with a chosen letter shift. Circulate to support them and check understanding.

###### Reflection

* Ask students what happened when they changed the wheel position.
* Ask whether the same word always looks the same when the code changes.
* Ask why someone might want to hide information this way.

##### Step 3: Wrap up

* Collect scissors and shared materials, then let students keep their cipher wheels. Reinforce: “Changing the code changes the message, but the meaning stays the same.”

#### Key points

* Use a cipher wheel to encode and decode simple words.

#### Discussion questions

**2 minutes**

* Review key terms: encode, decode, code, message.

#### Notes

###### Classroom management

* Supervise scissor and pin use carefully.
* Encourage patience during the craft stage.

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Timed decoding challenge using new encoded words.
* Play a decoding challenge such as cipher hangman where the encoded word is written on the board.

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: Use shorter words, a fixed letter shift, and non-Bitcoin vocabulary.
* Older students: Choose and record their own letter shift for others to solve.
* ELL/Accessibility: visual alphabet guides and peer support.
* Safety: blunt scissors and monitored pin use.

###### Suggested assignment

Use your cipher wheel to write a short message for a parent or caregiver, then help them decode it.

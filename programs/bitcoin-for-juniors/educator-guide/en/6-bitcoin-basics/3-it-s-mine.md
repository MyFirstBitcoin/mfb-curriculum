# 6.4 It's Mine!

_Simulation, Game · Whole class, Small groups · 45 min_

In this activity, learners simulate Bitcoin mining using playing cards as hashes and vocabulary words as transactions. Teams flip cards to find matching pairs. When they find two identical cards, they say “It’s mine.” A valid pair gives them access to the mempool, where they collect one Bitcoin vocabulary word. Words are grouped into blocks and only count after confirmation. The number of cards in the mining pool represents difficulty and is adjusted between rounds. Final scores are calculated by counting the total number of letters in confirmed words stored in each team’s wallet.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Explain that mining requires repeated attempts
* Describe how difficulty can increase or decrease
* Understand that transactions are grouped into blocks
* Recognize that confirmation comes before ownership
* Connect effort to reward

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “I find a matching pair and say ‘It’s mine’ to earn a word for my wallet.”

#### Materials

* 1–2 decks of playing cards
* 40–60 Bitcoin vocabulary word cards (See Appendix A)
* 1 envelope per team labeled WALLET
* 1 large envelope labeled BLOCK
* Signs labeled MEMPOOL and MINING POOL
* Timer
* Table or floor space for laying out cards
* Nice-to-have: Stickers or stamps for confirmation, Whiteboard for recording scores
* Nice-to-have: Bell or signal sound for block completion

#### Setup

* Set up a face-down card grid for the mining pool and mark separate MEMPOOL and BLOCK areas.
* Give each team a WALLET envelope and keep the word cards, timer, and extra playing cards ready.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

1. Briefly review [hash](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#hash), mine, block, wallet, and difficulty. Keep explanations short and concrete, then explain that students must “prove work” before earning words.

**5 minutes**

1. Lay out 20 playing cards face down in a grid and explain that two identical cards make a valid hash. Demonstrate flipping two cards, saying “It’s mine” for a match and replacing cards that do not match.

##### Step 2: Activity

**30 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Divide students into teams of 3–4 and give each team one WALLET envelope. Place word cards face down in the MEMPOOL area and the BLOCK envelope at the front.

###### Carry Out

1. Use 20 cards in the mining pool. Teams take turns flipping two; for a match, they say “It’s mine,” collect one MEMPOOL word, and read it correctly to keep it temporarily.
1. Stop the round after 6–8 minutes. If too many words were mined, add cards; if too few were mined, remove cards. Explain: “When mining is too fast, we make it harder. When mining is too slow, we make it easier.”

###### Carry Out

1. At the end of each round, collect earned words in the BLOCK envelope and announce “Block complete.” Return confirmed words to each team’s WALLET; only confirmed words count.

##### Step 3: Wrap up

* Count cards and return them to full decks. Gather word cards, empty wallets, and the BLOCK envelope; restack them, clear the mining area and board, and check for loose cards under tables and chairs.

#### Key points

* Simulate mining to explain difficulty, blocks, confirmation, and reward.

#### Discussion questions

**4 minutes**

* What happened when we added more cards?
* Was it harder to find pairs?
* Did you earn fewer words?
* Did longer words give higher scores?
* Did everyone follow the same rules?

#### Notes

###### Classroom management

*  Only one team flips at a time
* No touching cards out of turn
* Words must be read aloud clearly
* Cards must be returned neatly if unmatched

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Introduce a [halving](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#halving) event: after a set time, each valid hash earns only every second word
* Require red card pairs only for valid hashes
* Add a bonus if a team mines three pairs in one round

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: Using the word cards directly in the mining pool may make things easier.
* ELL/Accessibility: Add picture support to vocabulary cards. Allow peer reading assistance.
* Safety: Clear space around the mining pool. No running.

###### Suggested assignment

Explain the card-matching game to a parent or caregiver. Share how following the same rules helped the teams play together.

# 6.3 Satoshi, Where Are You?

_Creative writing, Discussion, Role play · Small groups, Individual · 60 min_

In this lesson, students construct a story using four independent ideas written on sticky notes. They begin by recalling the earlier story in which Satoshi disappeared and speculate about where he might have gone. Students then generate four ideas from guided prompts and organize them into a beginning, middle, and end structure. Through structured sharing and a sticky note swap, they revise their narratives to include new constraints. The lesson concludes with either a written or illustrated final draft, or a short dramatic role play using a class-generated story arc.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Sequence ideas into a beginning, middle, and end
* Construct a logical narrative from independent elements
* Adapt a story when new constraints are introduced
* Participate in structured peer storytelling
* Collaborate in a short dramatic performance

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “We are going to turn small ideas into a full story.”

#### Materials

* Sticky notes (4 per student)
* Markers or pencils
* Handout with three columns: Beginning / Middle / End (See Appendix A)
* Whiteboard
* Timer
* Blank writing paper and coloured pencils or crayons for the final-draft or three-panel option
* Nice-to-have: Coloured sticky notes
* Nice-to-have: Bell or signal for transitions

#### Setup

* Reserve board space for **Beginning / Middle / End** and set out markers and sticky notes.
* Arrange groups and have the timing signal ready.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**8 minutes**

1. Reference the earlier story where Satoshi disappeared, then ask:

* Where do you think Satoshi went?
* Why might he have left?
* What could have happened next?

1. Take brief responses, explain that students will build their own stories from connected small ideas, and ask one student to restate the task.

##### Step 2: Activity

**30 minutes**

###### Carry Out

1. Give each student four sticky notes and markers, explaining that each note holds one short idea. Students respond to these four prompts:

* What is your dream vacation destination?
* If you had one bitcoin, what would you do?
* What could turn a good day into a bad one?
* Who is your hero?

1. Circulate to keep responses concise and readable. Then distribute the three-column handout; students place their four notes in Beginning, Middle, or End, with more than one allowed in a column.
1. Students construct a short story using all four sticky notes, with:

* A clear beginning
* A change or problem in the middle
* An ending that shows what happened

1. In groups of 3–4, each student tells their story and listeners give one short comment about clarity or interest.
1. Each student passes one sticky note to the left, reorganizes their timeline to include the new idea, then revises and retells their story.

Optional: Repeat the swap once more if time allows.

###### Checkpoint

1. Ask: “What changed in your story?” and “Was it easier or harder to include the new idea?”

###### Production

**Option 1:** Students write a final draft (older learners) or draw a three-panel version with captions (younger learners).

**Option 2:** Collect sticky notes and randomly build a class story arc on the board. In small groups, students create a two-minute role play using all board elements. After performances, briefly identify the turning point and ending in each.

##### Step 3: Wrap up

* Collect sticky notes, handouts, and markers; clear the board and materials, reset the classroom, and store or submit student work appropriately.

#### Key points

* Build a coherent story with a beginning, middle, and end using prompts.

#### Discussion questions

**2 minutes**

1. Review and consolidate learning.
1. Ask:

* Why does order matter in a story?
* What makes a middle strong?
* What changed from your first version to your final version?

#### Notes

###### Classroom management

* Distribute sticky notes only at the moment of writing.
* Set visible time limits for each phase.
* Keep group sizes small to ensure participation.

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Require dialogue in the final draft.
* Add a secret object that must appear in the middle.
* Introduce a rule that the hero cannot appear in the beginning.

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: Allow drawing before writing and provide sentence starters.
* ELL/Accessibility: Provide vocabulary support and allow oral storytelling before written production.
* Safety: Keep movement controlled during role play.

###### Suggested assignment

Share your Satoshi story or picture with a parent or caregiver. Explain what happened at the beginning, middle, and end.

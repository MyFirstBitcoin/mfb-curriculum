# 3.3 Vote With Your Feet

_Debate, Experiential · Whole class · 20–25 min_

Students physically position themselves in the classroom to show whether they prefer older or newer technologies. By moving, debating, and explaining their choices, learners compare analogue and digital tools, consider trade-offs, and practice respectful disagreement. The activity reinforces prior learning on technological evolution while increasing engagement through movement.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Compare old and new technologies and explain preferences
* Identify advantages and disadvantages of different tools
* Practice expressing opinions and listening to others
* Engage in cooperative discussion through movement and debate

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “Show which technology I prefer and explain why.”

#### Materials

* Completed Analogue vs Digital matching worksheets (See 3.2 - Evolution of Technology)
* Three zone signs or floor markers labelled OLD, CENTER, and NEW
* Optional beads or stars for debate rewards
* Optional poster paper and markers
* Clear classroom space for movement
* Nice-to-have: Timer or bell for transitions

#### Setup

* Clear a safe movement area and mark three zones: OLD, CENTER, and NEW.
* Keep the completed worksheets and a timer or transition signal ready.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**3–5 minutes**

1. Have students retrieve and briefly review their completed Analogue vs Digital worksheet. Explain: “Now we’re going to share our opinions about old and new technologies,” and emphasize that there are no right or wrong answers.
1. Discuss whether new technologies are always better. Using one pair—such as typewriter versus laptop or horse and cart versus car—ask what each option does well, what might be lost in an upgrade, and which students initially prefer.

##### Step 2: Activity

**10-15 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Designate three physical zones—OLD, CENTER, and NEW—and make sure boundaries and movement rules are clear.

###### Model

1. Using an easy example such as letter versus email, model moving to a side, giving a short explanation, and listening respectfully to disagreement.

###### Carry Out

1. Read one pair of technologies, count down, and say “GO.” Students move to OLD, NEW, or CENTER based on preference.
1. Invite one or two students from each side to explain their choice, encourage respectful reasoning and disagreement, then return everyone to CENTER. Repeat with four to six pairs, or while engagement remains high.

###### Checkpoint

1. Observe whether students can give reasons beyond simple preference statements.
1. Pause to clarify misunderstandings about technologies when needed.

###### Reflection

* Ask whether anyone changed their mind after hearing from others.
* Ask which technology pair was hardest to choose.
* Reinforce that different preferences can still be valid.

##### Step 3: Wrap up

**2 minutes**

1. Have students return to their seats, remove signs, and reset the room. Thank them for respectful participation and reinforce: “Every technology has its time and place, and what matters is how we think about and use our tools.”

#### Key points

* Compare technologies and explain a personal preference.

#### Discussion questions

**3-5 minutes**

* Review that technology changes over time and serves different needs.
* Emphasize that both old and new tools have strengths.
* Connect the discussion to future lessons on innovation and adaptation.

Optional extensions include short debates, assigned arguments, or creating a class vote chart.

#### Notes

* Use this lesson to foster critical thinking _without judgment_
* Balance fun and focus - encourage physical activity while maintaining classroom order
* Consider using real or printed images of the items to support visual learners
* This activity can also segue into a future lesson about _innovation_, _change over time_, or _how people adapt to new tools_

###### Classroom management

* Enforce safe movement and clear boundaries
* Watch for quieter students and invite them to speak

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Visual bar graph of class preferences
* Journal reflection on favorite technology
* Instead of choosing, assign students a technology to argue _for_
* Create a class bar graph or poster

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: fewer rounds and simpler explanations.
* Older students: Invite 1–2 volunteers from each side to “defend” their choice
* ELL/Accessibility: visual cues, modeling, and sentence starters.
* Safety: no running and clear movement rules.

###### Suggested assignment

Tell a parent or caregiver which older or newer technology you chose and why. Ask which one they would choose.

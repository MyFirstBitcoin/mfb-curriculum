# 2.7 Smart Choices

_Discussion, Presentation, Craft, Game · Whole class, Small groups, Pairs, Individual · 60 min_

Students explore how everyday choices affect long-term outcomes by connecting time preference to food, health, work, play, and education. Through discussion, a visual time-preference axis, creative superhero design, and a lighthearted comparison game, learners reflect on how small daily decisions build strength, focus, and future success.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Understand that long-term thinking leads to stronger outcomes
* Apply [time preference](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#time-preference) to everyday decisions
* Reflect on habits related to health, food, learning, and work
* Recognize that small choices compound over time

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “Learn how my choices today can make me stronger later.”

#### Materials

* Superhero templates (See Appendix A)
* Time Preference (See Appendix B)
* Colouring pencils, crayons, or markers
* Beads or tokens for rewards
* Candy or small treats (optional)
* Hero Stat scoring guide (See Appendix C)
* Whiteboard or large poster space
* Markers
* Glue and scissors (optional)
* Nice-to-have: Timer or visual countdown clock

#### Setup

* Draw the time-preference axis on the board and set out the superhero templates, colouring tools, and game materials.
* Ready the beads and optional treats; leave space for discussion and play.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**10 minutes**

1. Recap _The Three Little Pigs_ by asking which house lasted longest and why it was strongest.
1. Draw a horizontal time-preference line: label the left “High Time Preference — wants results now” and the right “Low Time Preference — willing to wait for better results,” then place straw, sticks, and bricks along it.

##### Step 2: Activity

**45 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Explain that everyday choices are like building materials: some are fast and fun but weak, while others take effort and last longer.

###### Model

1. Use an example such as candy versus a healthy meal; place both choices on the time-preference axis and explain why.

###### Carry Out

##### Step 3: Discussion: Everyday Choices

**20 minutes**

1. Introduce the categories—food, health, work, play, and education—and give examples such as candy versus vegetables or rushing homework versus doing it well.
1. Ask students to place each choice on the axis and explain their reasoning.

##### Step 4: Design Your Superhero

**25 minutes**

1. Hand out superhero templates. Students name and colour their hero and choose favourites in each category; then review their choices and assign strength points based on time preference.

##### Step 5: Superhero Battle and Reflection

**15 minutes**

1. Pair students or form small groups to compare hero stats in Top-Trumps-style rounds; emphasize that the goal is learning, not winning.

###### Checkpoint

1. Observe whether students can explain why certain choices create stronger heroes.
1. Listen for connections between effort and long-term outcomes.

###### Reflection

* Ask: “Which choices made your hero strongest?”
* Ask: “Is there one choice you want to improve?”
* Invite students to name one low time preference action they could try this week.

##### Step 6: Wrap up

**5 minutes**

1. Collect materials, clean up craft areas, and thank students for their creativity and honesty. Reinforce: “Strong futures are built one small choice at a time.”

#### Key points

* Apply long-term thinking to make stronger everyday choices.

#### Discussion questions

**10 minutes**

1. Review the idea that small daily choices add up over time.
1. Reinforce that effort now often leads to better results later.
1. Connect to real life by asking students to identify one habit they can practice today.

#### Notes

###### Classroom management

* Keep tone encouraging and non-judgmental
* Emphasize that habits are explored, not graded

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Redesign the hero after one week of better choices
* Delay-of-gratification challenge using a small treat and a bonus reward for waiting

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: fewer choices and guided selection
* ELL/Accessibility: visuals, modeling, buddy support
* Safety: supervise scissors and movement

###### Suggested assignment

Talk with a parent or caregiver about one small choice that can help someone later. Share one example from the activity.

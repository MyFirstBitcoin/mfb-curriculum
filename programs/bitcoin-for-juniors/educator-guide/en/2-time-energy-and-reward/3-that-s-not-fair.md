# 2.4 That's Not Fair!

_Game, Discussion, Experiential · Whole class, Small groups, Pairs, Individual · 60 min_

Students explore the idea of fairness by participating in a sequence of short games that distribute rewards in different ways. Through direct experience, learners compare unfair systems, random systems, effort-based systems, and Proof of Work. Emotional responses are welcomed and used to support structured reflection. The activity builds a foundational understanding of fairness, reward, and equal opportunity.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Experience and identify unfair, random, effort-based, and fair systems
* Understand that not all rewards are earned equally
* Recognize that fairness requires equal rules and equal opportunity
* Build vocabulary and emotional awareness around effort, reward, and fairness
* Prepare conceptually for [Proof of Work](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#proof-of-work)

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “Test out which games are fair and which ones aren’t, and why.”

#### Materials

* Beads or small tokens to represent rewards (“My Stack”), with at least one per student and a small reserve
* Flashcards or images showing qualities or attributes (hair colour, clothing, birthdays, etc.)
* Drawing paper and pencils
* Five dice per student for the Proof of Work round
* Deck of playing cards
* Materials for the selected work challenge (for example, puzzle cards, sorting objects, or question cards)
* Timer or stopwatch
* Central table or container to act as the “bank”
* Whiteboard or large chart paper and marker for recording class verdicts
* Open space for movement-based games
* Nice-to-have: Visual support (See Appendix A), labels for each round (Unfair, Random, Work, Proof of Work)

#### Setup

* Clear a safe play area and place all beads in a central “bank.”
* Label each round and arrange its materials in the order you will use them.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**5 minutes**

1. Explain: “We will play four games; after each, you decide whether it was fair.” Introduce **My Stack**: students keep beads only from rounds the class accepts as fair.
1. Ask what fair means and how unfairness feels.

##### Step 2: Activity

**45 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Explain: vote after every round. Put beads from a round the class calls unfair back in the bank; repeat a fair round if helpful.

###### Model

1. Model earning and returning a bead, plus respectful responses to winning, losing, and disagreement.

##### Step 3: Round 1: Unfair

**10 minutes**

1. Use an openly unfair method (for example, teacher preference or arbitrary traits). Vote: equal chance? Did effort matter? Return the beads.

##### Step 4: Round 2: Random

**10 minutes**

1. Reward by dice or cards. Ask whether luck and effort made it fair; return beads if the class rejects it.

##### Step 5: Round 3: Work

**10 minutes**

1. Run a physical or mental challenge. Ask whether everyone could participate equally; return beads unless the class accepts the system.

##### Step 6: Round 4: Proof of Work

**10 minutes**

1. Give each student five dice and a clear target, such as three matching numbers. Students keep rolling until they succeed, then decide whether the same rules for everyone made it fair. If so, add the rewards to **My Stack**.

Optional: assign a rotating student to verify results before rewards are confirmed.

###### Checkpoint

1. Record each class verdict.

###### Reflection

* Ask how the games felt and what made the last one different. Highlight fair rules, equal chances, and checking results.

##### Step 7: Wrap up

**5 minutes**

1. Tidy up. Reinforce: “Fair systems give everyone the same rules and chance.”

#### Key points

* Compare reward systems to explain what makes a system fair.

#### Discussion questions

**10 minutes**

1. Review the four systems experienced: unfair, random, work-based, Proof of Work.
1. Discuss why Proof of Work allowed everyone the same chance.
1. Connect the experience to real-world systems where rewards are earned.

**Optional story or creative reflection**: students draw or write about a time something felt unfair or a system they think is fair.

#### Notes

###### Classroom management

* Acknowledge emotions openly and normalize frustration.
* Maintain calm control of bead distribution.
* Rotate roles to ensure inclusivity.

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Retell the activity as a story or fable.
* Compare fairness in games vs fairness in real life.

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: fewer dice or shared rolls.
* ELL/Accessibility: visual cues, modeling, buddy system.
* Safety: supervise physical challenges and allow alternatives.

###### Suggested assignment

Tell a parent or caregiver about one game rule that felt fair to you. Ask them what helps make a rule fair.

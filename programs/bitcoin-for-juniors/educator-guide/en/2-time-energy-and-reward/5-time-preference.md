# 2.6 Time Preference

_Story time, Discussion · Whole class · 20 min_

Students explore the concept of time preference through a familiar story, _The Three Little Pigs_. By listening to the story, discussing character choices, and comparing building materials, learners reflect on short-term convenience versus long-term strength. The activity helps children understand that effort and patience can lead to better outcomes later.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Recognize that choices made quickly can have consequences later
* Understand that effort and patience can lead to stronger outcomes
* Begin to grasp the idea of time preference in simple terms
* Practice comparing options and explaining their reasoning

**Objective In Student’s Words**

*  “Listen to the story and decide which choices were smart and why.”

#### Materials

* Storybook or printed visual version of The Three Little Pigs
* Visual aids showing houses made of straw, sticks, and bricks (See Appendix A)
* Printed straw–sticks–bricks comparison sheet (one per student or pair)
* Crayons or markers
* Whiteboard or poster board for group discussion notes
* Markers
* Nice-to-have: Puppets or character cut-outs
* Nice-to-have: Projector or screen for displaying images

#### Setup

* Place the storybook and visual aids where students can see them.
* Ready the board for ideas and set out handouts and crayons.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**3 minutes**

1. Recall _The Three Little Pigs_ and the houses. Explain that the class will consider each pig’s choices.

##### Step 2: Activity

**15 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Explain that students will use the story and pictures to discuss smart choices; remind them to take turns.

###### Model

1. Model: “The easy way can feel good now; the strong way can help later.”

###### Carry Out

1. Read with visuals; pause to predict and compare how quickly each house is built and how it stands up to the wolf.
1. Discuss the strongest, fastest, and most durable house. Define **time preference**: choosing something easy now or something better later.
1. Complete the straw–sticks–bricks comparison (_Appendix A_) and ask which house students would build.

###### Reflection

* Ask which material lasts longest, which is quickest, and when waiting helped them.

##### Step 3: Wrap up

**2 minutes**

1. Tidy up. Reinforce: “Working harder now can help later.”

#### Key points

* Use a story to compare short-term ease with long-term outcomes.

#### Discussion questions

**3-5 minutes**

* Review the main idea: short-term choices can cause long-term problems.
* Apply time preference to everyday decisions such as saving treats or finishing work before playing.
* Reinforce that patience and effort can lead to better results.

#### Notes

If teachers prefer, other stories can be selected for reading. The focus of this activity is to discuss the importance of how time preference can affect outcomes.

###### Classroom management

* Keep storytelling interactive with predictions and gestures
* Use pauses to manage attention and engagement

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Use pauses to manage attention and engagement
* Discuss materials used today such as plastic, wood, leather, or stone

###### Differentiation

* Younger students: pointing, visuals, yes/no questions
* ELL/Accessibility: repetition, images, simplified language
* Safety: seated activity, minimal movement

###### Suggested assignment

Tell a parent or caregiver which little pig made the strongest choice and why. Ask what patient choice has helped them in the past.

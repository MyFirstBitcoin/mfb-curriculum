# Introduction

This guide explains how to read and use the lesson plans in Bitcoin for Juniors. It walks through each section of the lesson plan format so educators know what to expect, how to prepare, and how to adapt the content for their context. The structure is here to serve you — not the other way around.

##### Using the Lesson Plans

Each lesson plan in Bitcoin for Juniors is a structured guideline. It is not a script that must be followed word for word. Teachers are encouraged to adapt, simplify, expand, or modify activities based on their students, setting, and time constraints. The plans are designed to support clarity and confidence, especially for educators who are new to teaching money or Bitcoin concepts.

Adjustments are expected. Facilitate rather than lecture. The framework provides structure. The teacher provides judgment and context.

##### Understanding the Structure

Each activity follows a consistent format. This allows teachers to quickly identify what is needed and how to run the session effectively.

###### Activity title

The activity title identifies the session and its place in the program. It appears at the top of every activity.

###### Keywords

Keywords identify the main ideas and vocabulary in the activity. Use them to find activities that connect to a topic you are teaching or to preview terms that may need introducing before the session.

###### Activity type

Activities are categorized for ease of planning. For example: simulation game, guided discussion, experiential learning, cooperative challenge. These labels help teachers select activities that match their instructional goals and classroom energy levels.

###### Participation

Participation indicates how students will take part in the activity, such as whole-class work, pairs, small groups, or individual work. It helps establish the intended learning dynamic and informs how you arrange the room and materials.

###### Duration

The time listed is approximate. Some groups will move quickly. Others will require more discussion. Teachers should treat duration as a flexible guide rather than a strict limit.

###### Objective

Each activity has one objective, stated as a single, focused outcome. It defines what students should know, understand, or be able to do by the end of the activity. If time is limited, prioritize the objective over completing every step.

###### Description

The description provides a brief overview of the activity: what students will do and how the experience supports the objective. Read it first to decide whether the activity suits your group and setting.

###### Preparation

Preparation has two parts:

* **Materials** lists the items needed to run the activity, including student-facing resources and teacher supplies.
* **Setup** explains what to arrange, prepare, or decide before beginning. Complete these steps in advance when possible to support smooth transitions.

###### Procedure

The procedure is organized into numbered steps: **Step 1**, **Step 2**, and so on. Each step describes a logical action in the session. Read the full sequence before class, then adapt the pacing, examples, and level of support to your students.

###### Review

Review helps students consolidate the learning after the activity. It includes:

* **Key points** — the main ideas to reinforce.
* **Discussion questions** — prompts for reflection, conversation, and connections to students' experience.

###### Notes

Notes offer practical guidance for adapting the activity to your classroom. They may include suggestions for classroom management, extensions or sponge activities, and differentiation. Use the suggestions that are useful for your learners and context.

###### Additional resources

Additional resources link to supporting materials, templates, printable resources, or further reading connected to the activity. Review any resources you plan to use before class so that they are ready when needed.

###### Suggested assignment

Each activity ends with an optional, non-evaluative suggested assignment. These are simple ways for learners to connect the session to home: they may share something they made, explain an idea, repeat a small activity, or ask a parent or caregiver a question. No written response, correct answer, or assessment is expected. The aim is simply to extend the conversation and give families a small window into the course.

##### Final Guidance

These lesson plans are structured for clarity, but flexibility is expected. Focus on the objective. Allow space for student voice. Prioritize interaction over perfection.

Bitcoin for Juniors is designed to support confident teaching and meaningful learning.

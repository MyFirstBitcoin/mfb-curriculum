# 1.4 Ship Rekt

_Simulation, Role play, Game · Whole class · 60 min_

In this whole-class simulation, students roleplay as shipwrecked survivors who must secure essential resources to “survive” a week on a desert island. Using playing cards to represent tools and supplies, learners begin with pure barter, practicing negotiation while encountering common trade barriers such as surplus, scarcity, and the coincidence of wants. A timed “Shell Hunt” then introduces commodity money, as students collect tokens that can be used to simplify exchange. With shells in circulation, the class enters a guided marketplace where students set prices, respond to supply and demand, and experience price discovery. The activity culminates in budgeting and either a short trade round (the 60-minute core route) or a structured auction (the 70-minute advanced route) for “treasure” wishlist items, bridging to why money exists.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

* Students experience [barter](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#barter), the “coincidence of wants,” [scarcity](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#scarcity), supply/demand, [price](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) discovery, and budgeting. The advanced route also introduces auctions.
* Bridge to money: [commodity money](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#commodity-money) (shells) and why money helps trade.

**Objective In Student’s Words**

* Trade objects to survive a week on the desert island.

#### Materials

* Playing cards (at least three per student, organized into suit-based supply piles)
* “Shells” (physical tokens: paper circles, buttons, craft shells)
* Treasure chest (small box or envelope)
* Gift card (expired or fake $100 gift card)
* Blank flash cards (or slips of paper)
* Trade-grid sheets or a large trade grid displayed where everyone can use it
* Chart paper or board space and markers for the shell-count bar graph
* One low-cost or pretend wishlist item per student for the final trade or auction route
* Timer/phone stopwatch
* Whiteboard or screen
* Marker pen
* Nice-to-have: Visual aids (See Appendices A & B), treasure map, reward stickers
* Nice-to-have: Projector, bell/signal for time, music for the treasure hunt, labelled containers for the used-card pile and shells

#### Setup

* Set out the playing cards and hide the shells around the room before students arrive.
* Keep the treasure chest, gift card, and timer ready for the later rounds.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set the scene

**3 minutes**

* Briefly review _trade_, _money_, and _price_, plus the class signal for stopping and the time limit.

**5 minutes**

1. Tell a brief shipwreck story: the crew reaches an island and must find what they need to survive. Use _Appendix A_ if helpful.
1. Ask students to name four useful supplies—machete, fishing rod, tent, and matches—and explain that playing cards represent them.
1. Check that everyone understands the goal.

##### Day 1 — Barter

**9 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Give each student several cards of one suit: diamonds = machetes, hearts = fishing rods, clubs = tents, spades = matches.
1. As each student explains one use, move one card to the used pile. Ask what they can do with extras; guide them to trading for what they need (_Appendix B_).

###### Model

* Show one simple trade: one student makes an offer, the other responds, and they agree. Add sentence frames if helpful: “I have ____. I need ____.”

###### Trade

* Explain the trade grid, then give students five minutes to trade. Use the class signal to stop.

###### Checkpoint & Debrief

* Thumbs up/side/down: met needs, almost, or not yet. Discuss who had too much or too little; introduce **scarcity** and **supply and demand**.

###### Reflection

* Prompt students: “One thing that helped me trade was ______.”

##### Day 2 — Barter again

**9 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Quickly review the suits and their supplies. Move one used card per student, then invite another round of trading for needs.

###### Model

* Repeat the short trade model and sentence frames if needed.

###### Trade

* Use the trade grid for another five-minute trading round; use the class signal to stop.

###### Checkpoint & Debrief

* Repeat the thumbs check and discuss what made this round hard.

###### Mini-Lesson

* Point out the problem: trading is hard when two people do not want each other’s items. Explain that money can help.

##### Day 3 — Scarcity and effort: Shell Hunt

**9 minutes**

###### Setup

* Hide shells if needed. Explain that they can be used as money; set safety rules (no pushing or taking).

###### Treasure Hunt

* Run a five-minute shell hunt (2–3 shells per student, with varied difficulty).

###### Count & Record

* Pairs count shells; make a quick many/few bar graph and discuss **scarcity** and **effort**.

###### Mini-Lesson

* Define **commodity money**—a thing people use as money—and ask whether shells could work on the island.

###### Reflection

* “If shells become money, what might the **price** of items be? Why?”

##### Day 4 — Commodity money and prices: Shell Market

**9 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Review the supplies, move one used card per student, and explain that shells may now be used in trades.

###### Model

* Model a trade with shells. Students decide the prices.

###### Trade

* Use the trade grid for five minutes; use the class signal to stop.

###### Checkpoint & Debrief

* Use the thumbs check. Ask what money made easier or harder, what prices emerged, and whether the result felt fair.

##### Day 5 — Treasure Chest and Wishlist

**7 minutes**

###### Prep

* Hide the treasure chest during Day 4.

###### Story Hook

* Announce that the crew has found treasure, including a pretend gift card.

###### Treasure hunt

* Students find the chest; explain that it will fund one luxury wish each.

###### Wishlist

* Record one luxury wish beside each student’s name; tell the class the items arrive tomorrow.

##### Day 6 — Wishlist

Choose one final-round option. Option 1 is the 60-minute core route; Option 2 is the 70-minute advanced route.

###### OPTION 1 – Random distribution and Trade (SIMPLE)

**5 minutes**

Randomly distribute the wish-list items (for example, an airdrop scattered them). Students trade items and remaining shells to get what they want.

###### Trade Prep

* Remind students that they set prices and trade what they have for what they want.

###### Trade

* Allow five minutes of trading. Students may include shells and playing cards; use the class signal to stop.

###### OPTION 2 – Auction (ADVANCED)

**15 minutes**

* The finder owns the items; sell them by **auction** for shells.

###### Auction Prep (3 min)

* Explain an **English auction**: prices rise and the last bidder wins. Practice signals and remind students not to exceed their shells.

###### Auctions (9 min)

* Auction items one at a time.

###### Auction Debrief (3 min)

* Ask how budgeting, demand, and rules affected the result.

##### Optional extension: Day 7 — Freedom

* In Think–Pair–Share, use the available objects to plan an escape.

**4 minutes**

* What items were **expensive**? Why? Where did **supply** feel low? What strategies worked?
* “Money made trading easier because ______.”
* Barter was hard → shells helped (commodity money) → money solves coincidence of wants.
* Bridge to Bitcoin – discuss that Bitcoin is a money that helps people exchange goods and services, then discuss one difference between Bitcoin and shells.
* Gather materials and verify that all playing cards have been returned for equal distribution in a future activity.

#### Key points

* Barter can be difficult when people do not want what the other person has.
* Commodity money can make exchange easier.
* Prices, budgets, supply, and demand affect trading decisions.

#### Discussion questions

* Quick write or drawing: “One thing I learned about **trade** or **money**.”
* Gallery walk of price tags & auction results; discuss fairness and markets.
* Optional challenge (older kids): Design a new island money (rules, supply cap, how to prevent cheating).

#### Notes

###### Classroom management

* Set “Marketplace” boundaries; use a call-back (e.g., “Bit, block… BOOM!”).
* Trading rules posted: 1) Be kind, 2) Offer/Counteroffer, 3) No grabbing, 4) Finalize by handshake/high-five, 5) Return to places on call-back.
* If required, assign roles (Banker/Pirate helpers, Market Monitors) to channel energy and support younger learners.

###### Extensions and sponge activities

* **Draw your island store** and price three items in shells.
* **Mental math**: If you have 12 shells and win an auction for 9 shells, what’s left?
* Make a **Venn diagram** to show what kids [value](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#value) at home and on the island - are there objects which fall into both?
* **Top 10 Wishlist** - ask students to write a list of the top 10 items they would pack if they were going to a desert island.

###### Differentiation

* Younger students trade fewer categories (just Food/Water); older students handle more complex needs and dynamic pricing.
* ELL/Accessibility: Provide picture cards; sentence frames for offers; assign peer buddies.
* Safety: Movement is walking only; clear boundaries; timed rounds.

###### Suggested assignment

Explain barter to a parent or caregiver. Share how using shells made trading easier in the activity.

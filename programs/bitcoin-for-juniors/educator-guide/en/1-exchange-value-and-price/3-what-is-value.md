# 1.3 What is value?

_Discussion, Craft · Whole class, Individual · 45 min_

Students explore the concept of value by identifying everyday items from their own lives and positioning them on a shared visual chart. Through guided discussion and a collaborative wall-based activity, learners compare personal value with price and reflect on how value can change depending on perspective. The activity builds foundational understanding of value, price, and scarcity through discussion, movement, and visual thinking.

#### Objectives

**Learning Outcomes**

By the end of this activity, learners will:

* Understand that _value_ is based on personal and social needs.
* Recognize the relationship between [_price_](https://curriculum.myfirstbitcoin.org/lexicon/en/#price) and _value_.
* See that value is not fixed but changes depending on perspective.
* Practice collaboration through shared discussion and group art.

**Objective In Student’s Words**

* “Find out what decides the price of things.”
* “Learn what things are valuable to us and why not everyone agrees.”
* “Talk about why expensive things aren’t always valuable, and cheap things can still matter.”

#### Materials

* Sticky notes or index cards
* Markers, pens or pencils
* Tape or sticky putty
* Low-tack tape (masking tape, washi tape, painter’s tape, etc.)
* Chart paper for the four-quadrant display when a board is unavailable
* Nice-to-have: A selection of objects from home
* Nice-to-have: Whiteboard & markers

#### Setup

* Clear a wall or whiteboard for the shared display.
* Put the sticky materials and any optional household objects at the front of the room.

#### Procedure

##### Step 1: Set up the lesson

**5 minutes**

1. Show the objects and ask what they cost and why they matter.
1. Define:
1. **Price**: how much something costs
1. **Value**: how useful or important something is
1. Invite different opinions about the same object.

##### Step 2: Activity

**30-40 minutes**

###### Setup

1. Students write one household item per sticky note. Prompt: “Which things matter most to you?”
1. Draw a four-quadrant chart (_Appendix B_): Low–High Price across; Low–High Value up.

###### Model

1. Place one example on the chart and explain your choice.

###### Carry out

1. Students place their notes, then add or move items as their thinking changes.

###### Reflection

* Ask: “Is it cheap or expensive? Is it useful? Would everyone agree?”
* Compare a few placements, including a cheap/high-value and expensive/low-value item.
* Reinforce: price is a number; value is a judgment.

##### Step 3: Wrap up

1. Notice one pattern in the chart, then tidy or display it for later.

#### Key points

* Compare price and value to see how people may value things differently.

#### Discussion questions

* Price and value are not the same
* People disagree on value
* Prices help people trade even when they disagree
* Bridge to money:
  * Money and prices help us trade when value is subjective

#### Notes

###### Classroom management

* Encourage respectful disagreement
* Reinforce that there are no wrong answers
* It can be more engaging to tape out and label the axes during the activity. This could also distract students and break their concentration if the process is complicated.

###### Extensions & Sponge Activities

* Show and Tell
* Add a third layer: “Who decides the price?”
* Re-introduce auctions or trading games

###### Differentiation

* **Younger students**: pre-printed item cards
* **ELL/Accessibility**: drawing instead of writing
* **Safety**: supervise movement near walls

###### Suggested assignment

Choose something at home that you value. Tell a parent or caregiver why it matters to you, then ask whether they would value it in the same way.
